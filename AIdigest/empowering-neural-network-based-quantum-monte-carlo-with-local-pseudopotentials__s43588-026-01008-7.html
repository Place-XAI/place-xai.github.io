Empowering neural network-based quantum Monte Carlo with local pseudopotentials

Weizhong Fu · Ryunosuke Fujimaru · Ruichen Li · Yuzhi Liu · Xuelan Wen · Xiang Li · Kenta Hongo · Liwei Wang · Tom Ichibha · Ryo Maezono · Ji Chen · Weiluo Ren Published 2026-07-10 DOI 10.1038/s43588-026-01008-7 Source nature.com ↗
Core Conclusion

Pairing neural network-based quantum Monte Carlo (NNQMC) with local pseudopotentials / pseudo-Hamiltonians (PH) removes core electrons from the neural wavefunction and, counterintuitively, delivers better relative-energy accuracy than all-electron (AE) NNQMC on complex systems — not merely a cheaper approximation. Because the local (as opposed to semilocal ECP) form avoids costly non-local integration terms, it is also substantially faster than the standard ECP route. The combination makes previously out-of-reach systems tractable, demonstrated on the Fe4S4(SCH3)4 iron–sulfur cluster, and thereby widens the practical scope of accurate ab initio calculation.

★

Why It Matters

  • Breaks the size ceiling of NNQMC. Neural wavefunctions (FermiNet/DeepMind-lineage ansätze) reach near-exact energies but scale steeply with electron count; freezing chemically inert core electrons into a local pseudopotential attacks the dominant cost driver directly rather than trimming constants.
  • Overturns an "approximation must cost accuracy" intuition. Removing electrons improves relative energies here, because the neural network no longer has to spend capacity representing sharp, high-frequency core cusps — a network-specific effect (cf. the frequency-principle literature the paper cites) with no analogue in classical basis-set QMC.
  • Local beats semilocal on cost. Standard effective core potentials (ECPs) are semilocal and force expensive angular-quadrature evaluation at every Monte Carlo step plus the locality-approximation bias; a purely local pseudo-Hamiltonian sidesteps both.
  • Unlocks a genuinely hard target. Fe4S4 cubanes are the canonical stress test for strongly correlated, near-degenerate transition-metal chemistry (nitrogenase/electron-transfer biology) — a system class where DFT is unreliable and exact methods historically unaffordable.
  • Ships as usable infrastructure. The PH implementation is released in ByteDance's open JaQMC repository with a version-pinned Zenodo snapshot, so the speed-up is reproducible rather than reported.

Core Methods

  • Neural network wavefunction ansatz: a deep fermionic neural network (FermiNet/Psiformer lineage) parameterizes the many-electron wavefunction, optimized variationally with KFAC second-order updates and accelerated by the authors' forward-Laplacian framework for high-order differential operators.
  • Local pseudo-Hamiltonian (PH) substitution: core electrons are replaced by a purely local effective potential in the Bachelet–Ceperley pseudo-Hamiltonian family (position-dependent effective mass), so the Hamiltonian stays a local operator that the Monte Carlo estimator can evaluate pointwise.
  • Avoiding semilocal integration: conventional ECPs are angular-momentum projected (semilocal), requiring quadrature over projector integrals at every walker step and the DMC locality/T-move machinery; PH eliminates these terms — the source of the reported speed advantage over ECP.
  • Three-way controlled comparison: AE vs ECP vs PH benchmarked on the same NNQMC stack, separating efficiency (Fig. 2) from accuracy (Fig. 3) so the counterintuitive accuracy gain cannot be an artefact of different codes.
  • Relative-energy evaluation protocol: accuracy is judged on chemically meaningful differences (atomization/ionization/excitation-type quantities) against experiment and high-level references, which is where systematic core errors cancel and where the PH advantage appears.
  • Variational + diffusion Monte Carlo: the neural ansatz is used both variationally and as a fixed-node DMC trial function (the group's prior NNQMC-DMC line), so the PH treatment must remain consistent under projection.
  • Generalizability sweep: PH constructions extended beyond sulfur to phosphorus- and chlorine-containing clusters and transition-metal systems, then applied to Fe4S4(SCH3)4, where broken-symmetry / spin-coupling analysis (Noodleman-type) frames the target quantities.

① Learn the local pseudopotential end-to-end instead of constructing it by hand

Extending this paperThe PHs here are constructed offline, element by element (a separate team task per the author contributions) and then handed to the neural wavefunction as a fixed operator. That is a two-stage pipeline with a hand-tuned interface. A direct extension: make the local pseudo-Hamiltonian differentiable and co-optimized with the ansatz — parameterize the position-dependent effective mass and local potential as a small network per element, train it against AE references on a few small systems, then freeze and transfer. This would test whether the paper's accuracy gain is a property of their particular PH or of the local-operator class in general, and it would turn per-element construction cost (currently a bottleneck for extending beyond S, P, Cl and 3d metals) into a one-off learning problem. The interatomic-potential community has already industrialized exactly this "learn the effective operator" step.

Added value to my researchThis is the strongest template I have seen for replacing a hand-specified spatial kernel with a learned local operator. In 2SFCA/R2SFCA the distance-decay function and the catchment radius play precisely the role of a pseudopotential: they are the analyst's device for summarizing everything happening below the scale we model explicitly. A "learned local operator, frozen and transferred" recipe gives me a defensible way to fit decay/catchment operators once on data-rich cities (with mobile-phone or GPS ground truth) and transfer them to data-poor regions, instead of re-tuning a Gaussian per study area.

② Formalize the "freed capacity" mechanism into a spectral budget theory

Extending this paperThe paper's headline surprise — fewer electrons yielding better relative energies — is explained qualitatively by the distinctive characteristics of neural ansätze, leaning on the frequency-principle results it cites (networks learn low frequencies first and struggle with sharp high-frequency structure such as core cusps). That explanation is currently a narrative, not a predictive rule. Extending the paper: measure the spectral content of the wavefunction that is actually removed when a core is frozen, and relate it to the observed accuracy change, producing an a-priori criterion — given an element and a target property, how many electrons should be pseudized before the trade turns negative? That would convert a case-by-case empirical finding into a design rule, and would predict where PH must fail (systems whose chemistry genuinely involves semicore states).

Added value to my researchThis reframes the Modifiable Areal Unit Problem in a way I can actually operationalize. Micro-scale spatial noise — individual building footprints, single POI jitter, one-off trajectory spikes — is high-frequency structure that consumes model capacity that should be spent on the policy-relevant meso-scale accessibility gradient. A spectral-budget diagnostic gives an objective criterion for aggregation level and for MGWR/KDE bandwidth, replacing cross-validation-by-habit with "resolve the frequencies your target quantity depends on, coarse-grain the rest".

③ Let uncertainty decide where the expensive treatment is spent

Extending this paperRight now the AE/ECP/PH choice is a global, human decision applied uniformly to a system. But the paper's own results show the ranking is system-dependent (the PH advantage grows with complexity, and Fig. 4 exists precisely to test where it holds). Extending it: attach a calibrated uncertainty estimate to NNQMC-PH predictions — from ensembles of trained wavefunctions, from the local-energy variance already available for free in any Monte Carlo run, or both — and use it to trigger AE fall-back only on the atoms/regions where PH error is predicted to matter. That yields an adaptive-fidelity NNQMC that automates the accuracy/cost decision rather than delegating it to method-developer intuition, and it makes the Fe4S4 class of results auditable.

Added value to my researchDirectly reusable for my Monte Carlo and agent-based work: local-energy variance is a free per-sample error signal, and the analogous quantity in spatiotemporal KDE or an SIR/ABM run is the per-cell sampling variance. An uncertainty-driven scheduler that concentrates simulation budget on high-variance cells — rather than sampling uniformly over a study region — is a cheap, publishable methods contribution for telehealth-access and mental-health-accessibility simulations where budget is the binding constraint.

④ From per-system solver to a transferable pretrained wavefunction with a frozen core

Extending this paperEvery result here still requires optimizing a wavefunction from scratch for each system — the PH cuts the per-system cost but does not amortize across systems. Yet the paper has quietly built the ideal precondition for amortization: by pseudizing cores, all elements now present the network with a uniform, low-frequency valence problem. Extending it: pretrain a single transferable NNQMC-PH ansatz across the paper's whole P/Cl/S/3d chemical space and fine-tune per target, so that the marginal cost of a new Fe4S4-scale system falls further. This is the natural merger of the paper's contribution with the transferable-wavefunction line it already cites, and it is the step that would make NNQMC a routine tool rather than a heroic calculation.

Added value to my research"Freeze the invariant core, adapt the interacting valence" is exactly the fine-tuning discipline I want for geospatial foundation models: freeze the low-level spatial-representation blocks that encode universal geometry, adapt only the task/place-specific layer for a new city or health outcome. The paper also supplies the sharpest argument I have for why such freezing can improve rather than degrade downstream accuracy — a claim I currently make on efficiency grounds alone.

⑤ Close the multi-fidelity loop and propagate the error to the decision-relevant quantity

Extending this paperThe paper stops at energies. But its real value to the wider community is as a cheap generator of gold-standard reference data for systems where DFT is untrustworthy — precisely the strongly correlated transition-metal chemistry that machine-learning interatomic potentials are currently trained on with DFT labels. Extending it: run the full chain NNQMC-PH → labelled dataset → MLIP → molecular dynamics → observable property, and propagate the PH-induced error all the way through, so we learn whether a few mHa of pseudopotential bias survives or vanishes at the level of the property anyone actually reports. That would answer the question the paper's accuracy figures raise but cannot settle: accurate enough for what?

Added value to my researchThis is my multi-source data-fusion problem stated in a cleaner discipline: expensive high-accuracy sources (surveys, mobile-phone records, field validation) calibrate cheap broad-coverage sources (remote sensing, POIs, social media), and the honest question is whether fusion error survives propagation into the policy metric — an accessibility index, a service-area boundary, a siting decision. Adopting the paper's habit of reporting error on the relative, decision-relevant quantity rather than the absolute intermediate is a standard I should port into my FAIR/open-science reporting templates.
