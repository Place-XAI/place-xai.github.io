Universal cell embedding provides a foundation model for cell biology

Yanay Rosen · Yusuf Roohani · Ayush Agrawal · Leon Samotorčan · Stephen R. Quake · Jure Leskovec Published 2026-07-08 DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10689-z Source nature.com ↗
Core Conclusion

Universal Cell Embedding (UCE) is a self-supervised transformer foundation model that maps any single cell — from any tissue, any species, any protocol — into one shared 1,280-dimensional latent space with no labels, no retraining and no fine-tuning. The key trick is representing each gene not by an ID but by the ESM2 protein-language embedding of its protein product, which makes the model genome-agnostic and removes any dependence on homology mapping. Applied to build the Integrated Mega-scale Atlas (IMA) of 36 million cells, >1,000 named cell types, >300 datasets and eight species, the embedding space shows emergent structure it was never trained for — germ-layer lineages, Cell-Ontology-consistent distances, and correct cell-type transfer to species (green monkey, naked mole rat, chicken) absent from training. A single logistic classifier over these embeddings rediscovered kidney Norn cells and found Norn-like cells in lung and heart, including in IPF/COPD lungs.

★

Why It Matters

  • Zero-shot ends the per-dataset retraining tax. Every prior atlas-integration method (scVI, scArches, SAMap, SATURN) must be retrained on each new dataset, which mutates the representation space and makes results non-comparable across studies. UCE is used as is, so embeddings computed in different labs, years and species live in one shared, permanently comparable coordinate system.
  • Universality is achieved through the tokenizer, not through more data. By tokenizing genes as protein embeddings (ESM2-15B, 5,120-d) and grouping them by chromosome, UCE can encode any protein-coding gene of any species from sequence alone — no orthologue tables, no shared gene panel, no highly-variable-gene selection. This is the transferable design lesson far beyond cell biology.
  • Emergent biology is the evidence, not the metric. Trained only to predict whether a masked gene was expressed, the model recovers germ-layer organization (90/97 mesoderm-derived cell types nearest another mesoderm type), Cell Ontology tree distance monotonically up to five hops, and cross-tissue macrophage identity (human macrophages appear in 73 tissues; 72% of tissue-specific centroids are nearest another macrophage centroid).
  • It converts an atlas into a search engine for hypotheses. Because a classifier trained on 1,280-d embeddings can be applied directly to all 36M IMA cells, the Norn-cell case study runs an unbiased, annotation-independent sweep across tissues, species and diseases — a workflow that would otherwise require re-integrating every dataset.
  • Concrete quantitative gains. +13.9% overall, +16.2% biological conservation and +10.1% batch correction over the next-best zero-shot model (Geneformer) on Tabula Sapiens v.2; cross-atlas cell-type centroid alignment is 65% more accurate than in raw gene-expression space (92% when scored on the single nearest centroid).

Why Nature (Editorial Fit)

  • A universal representation, not another benchmark win. Nature favours claims of generality; the paper's thesis is a single latent space "spanning the universe of cell biology", validated on species the model never saw — a universality claim with a falsifiable test attached.
  • Emergent capability with mechanistic biological grounding. The AI story (self-supervision → emergence) is anchored to canonical biology (germ layers, Cell Ontology, marker genes), so the result reads as cell biology rather than as machine-learning engineering.
  • Discovery, not only representation. The Norn-cell study delivers an actual biological hypothesis — Norn-like, EPO-associated cells in lung and heart, with distinctive collagen and Egln1 signatures in IPF versus COPD — closing the loop from method to new biology to disease.
  • Infrastructure for a community-scale programme. UCE feeds directly into the Human Cell Atlas / virtual-cell agenda; weights, code (snap-stanford/uce) and embeddings (Zenodo) are open, making the paper a shared resource rather than a one-off analysis.
  • Honest about limits. The Discussion states that benchmarks only test recovery of subjective expert labels, that the model is a black box, that training data are mammal- and brain-biased, and that fine-grained transfer degrades at large evolutionary distance (Drosophila) — the kind of self-critique Nature-level review demands.

Core Data — Sources & Volume

Training corpus
The Integrated Mega-scale Atlas (IMA): over 36 million cells, more than 1,000 uniquely named cell types, more than 300 datasets, dozens of tissues, eight species (human, mouse, mouse lemur, zebrafish, pig, rhesus macaque, crab-eating macaque, western clawed frog).
Main source
CZ CELLxGENE Discover Census v.2023-07-10 supplies 33.9 million cells across 285 datasets (human + mouse), de-duplicated by keeping primary cells only. The remaining 2.3 million cells from 28 datasets cover the other species and were pulled from their original repositories (full list in the paper's Supplementary Table 16).
Gene tokens
ESM2 protein language model (15-billion-parameter variant) embeddings of amino-acid sequences, dimension dp = 5,120, averaged over all proteins encoded by a gene. Ablations showed ESM2-15B outperformed smaller protein LMs and random gene embeddings for under-represented species.
Preprocessing
For Census data: cells filtered at ≥200 genes, genes at ≥10 cells; no highly-variable-gene selection and no batch correction. Non-Census datasets were not preprocessed uniformly (the paper says so explicitly, Supplementary Note 1).
Working sample
For UMAPs, centroid matching and Norn prediction, a representative subsample of 2,969,114 cells was used (10,000 randomly drawn per dataset, or the whole dataset if smaller; mean 9,486 cells per dataset), with labels coarsened to 51 coarse cell types.
Primary benchmark
Tabula Sapiens v.2 — 581,430 cells, 27 tissues, 167 batches, 162 unique cell types, unpublished at model-training time. Ovary batch-effect test: 45,757 cells (10x 3′ v3.1) plus 3,610 cells (Smart-seq2.5).
Held-out species
Green monkey lung + mediastinal lymph node (GSE156755), naked mole rat spleen/circulating immune cells (GSE132642), chick retina (GSE159107), developing chick heart (GSE149457); Drosophila from the Fly Cell Atlas as an extreme-divergence probe.
Case-study data
Mouse kidney Norn-cell dataset (GSE193321) and human lung disease — IPF, COPD and controls (GSE136831).
Baseline space
The "original gene expression space" comparison uses the 5,704 genes shared across all 184 human IMA datasets, log-normalized per Scanpy defaults.
Ontology reference
Cell Ontology, release 16 August 2016; distance analysis on 100,000 randomly sampled cell pairs from Tabula Sapiens v.2.
Compute
33 layers, >650 million parameters, output embedding demb = 1,280, context of 1,024 sampled gene tokens, trained 40 days on 24 × A100 80 GB GPUs.

Core Methods

  • Cell as a "bag of RNA". Each cell is reduced to a multi-set of 1,024 genes sampled with replacement from its expressed genes, with sampling probability proportional to log-normalized expression — deliberately discarding exact quantitative levels in exchange for protocol and depth invariance.
  • Protein-embedding tokenization. Every sampled gene becomes the ESM2 embedding of its protein product (averaged over isoform products), compressed by a one-layer MLP. This is what makes the model genome-agnostic: a new species needs only its amino-acid sequences, no orthology mapping.
  • Genomic-position structure. Tokens are ordered by genomic location, grouped by chromosome between special chromosome start/end tokens (chromosome order randomized), forming a "cell sentence"; a CLS token prepended to the sequence yields the cell embedding at the final layer.
  • Self-supervised masked-expression objective. A fraction rmask (20% in the released model) of expressed genes is masked; the cell embedding is concatenated with each candidate gene embedding and an MLP predicts binary expressed/not-expressed under a binary cross-entropy loss balanced over equal numbers of masked-expressed and unexpressed genes. No cell-type or dataset labels are used at any point.
  • Zero-shot evaluation protocol. New datasets are embedded without any weight update and scored with the single-cell integration benchmark (bio-conservation + batch-correction), against zero-shot transformers (Geneformer, scGPT, tGPT) and fine-tuned methods (scVI, scArches) that see the target data.
  • Cross-species centroid matching. For each annotated cell type, a centroid is computed and matched to its nearest (and three-nearest) cell-type centroids in the IMA — the metric used to show 13/17 green-monkey, 17/24 naked-mole-rat and 12/15 chick-heart cell types align to the correct cross-species counterpart.
  • Structure audits against external knowledge. Hierarchical clustering dendrograms of embedded cell types; Euclidean-vs-Cell-Ontology-tree-distance regression with one-sided t-tests (P as low as 10−201); a germ-layer classifier on held-out cell types reaching >80% accuracy.
  • Downstream transfer by simple classifiers. A plain logistic classifier on frozen embeddings does the work — cross-species label transfer, and the Norn-cell sweep (98.3% label recapture in mouse kidney) applied unchanged to all 36M IMA cells, then validated by canonical marker-gene fold changes and pseudobulk differential expression across IPF/COPD/control.

Expanded Ideas — this paper's extensibility & added value to my research

Each idea stays anchored to this paper: first how the paper itself could be extended (drawing on our corpus), then the added value for my research lines (migis.net — GeoAI, spatial accessibility & health geography, explainable and physics-informed spatial learning, fine-tuning geospatial foundation models).

① Give UCE a context token: from dissociated cells to spatial neighbourhoods

Extending this paperUCE's core abstraction — a cell is a "bag of RNA" with 1,024 sampled gene tokens — deliberately throws away everything outside the cell membrane. Yet the paper's own best story is contextual: macrophages keep a shared identity across 73 tissues, Norn-like cells appear in lung and heart, and IPF Norn-like cells differ from COPD ones. A direct extension: append a small set of context tokens (spatial neighbours from spatial transcriptomics, niche composition, donor/disease state) to the cell sentence, and train the same masked-expression objective so the model learns cell state conditioned on its microenvironment. The zero-shot property survives because the context tokens, like the gene tokens, are built from a universal descriptor space rather than dataset-specific IDs.

Added value to my researchThis is exactly the representation problem in my urban analytics work: a place is a "bag of POIs" whose meaning depends on its neighbourhood. A UCE-style encoder over POI tokens (embedded by their text/semantic descriptors instead of a city-specific taxonomy code) plus context tokens for street-network position and population density yields a transferable place embedding — one coordinate system in which a neighbourhood in Wuhan and one in Boston can be compared for 15-minute accessibility or primary-care service-area delineation, without refitting per city.

② Turn the IMA from an atlas into a calibrated retrieval index

Extending this paperThe paper's headline weakness is buried in Fig. 3d: cross-atlas centroid alignment is exact for only 41% of cell types, and the authors attribute most misses to label-resolution differences rather than model error — but they have no way to tell the two apart. Extending the paper: wrap the 36M-cell IMA in an approximate-nearest-neighbour index and replace hard centroid matching with calibrated retrieval — return a ranked set of neighbours with a conformal confidence set over cell-type labels, and flag queries that fall outside the training manifold (the paper already shows shuffled-expression cells land out-of-distribution, so the signal exists). That converts "41% exact" into an honest, per-query uncertainty statement and makes the atlas usable as a production annotation service.

Added value to my researchThe same retrieval-plus-conformal pattern is what my accessibility work needs when transferring a 2SFCA/R2SFCA-calibrated model from a data-rich metro to a data-poor one: instead of silently extrapolating a distance-decay curve, retrieve the k most similar service areas in an embedding index and emit a prediction interval plus an out-of-support flag. It turns "does this model transfer?" from a hand-wave into a measurable per-tract quantity — directly relevant to telehealth and mental-health access mapping where quiet extrapolation is a policy hazard.

Cross-ref (corpus): A cell atlas foundation model for scalable search of similar human cells — 10.1038/s41586-024-08411-y

③ Stress-test "universal": a distance-stratified transfer benchmark

Extending this paperThe word "universal" is doing enormous work here, and the paper itself concedes the crack: coherent embeddings for Drosophila, but fine-grained cell-type transfer degrades across large evolutionary distances, and the training corpus is admittedly mammal- and brain-heavy. The extension is a proper ablation of universality as a function of distance: hold out entire clades and tissues, and fit performance against divergence time, protein-sequence identity, and training-corpus density, producing a transfer decay curve with an explicit competence boundary. That would replace a binary claim with a quantitative law — and would let users know, before running the model, whether their species is inside or outside the envelope.

Added value to my researchSpatial models fail the same way and it is almost never audited. I want the geospatial analogue: a transfer-decay curve for spatial foundation models, where the x-axis is not evolutionary distance but a composite of built-form dissimilarity, climate zone, income level and data-density — telling me in advance how far a model trained on North American cities can travel before its accessibility or urban-health predictions become unsupported. This pairs naturally with MGWR: local coefficient instability and embedding-space distance should be two readings of the same non-stationarity.

④ Open the 1,280 dimensions: mechanistic interpretability of a cell embedding

Extending this paperThe Discussion names the problem plainly — UCE "operates largely as a black box" and interpretability tools are needed to connect representations to mechanism. A concrete extension: train a sparse dictionary / overcomplete autoencoder on the 1,280-d embeddings of the IMA, then test whether the recovered latent directions align with pathways, cell-cycle phase, hypoxia response or germ-layer identity, and validate causally by ablating gene tokens and measuring embedding displacement. Because UCE is frozen and shared, such a dictionary becomes a reusable public interpretation layer for every downstream study — a much stronger contribution than per-paper attention-map inspection.

Added value to my researchMy explainable-ML-for-spatial-prediction line has the identical failure mode: SHAP over engineered features explains the wrapper, not the representation. Adapting this gives me concept-level explanations for geospatial embeddings — latent directions corresponding to "transit richness", "green exposure", "service desert" — which is what a health-policy audience actually needs, and which survives the classic Rudin critique better than post-hoc attribution on a black box.

⑤ Steal the tokenizer, not the weights: a sequence-style universal token space for places

Extending this paperThe single most portable idea in this paper is that universality was bought at the tokenizer: genes are represented by ESM2 embeddings of their protein products, so an unseen genome costs nothing. The extension inside cell biology is obvious and untried at scale — apply the same substitution to other entity vocabularies (regulatory elements by DNA-LM embeddings, metabolites by structure embeddings, perturbations by compound embeddings) and check whether the resulting model inherits the same zero-shot property, i.e. test whether "descriptor-space tokenization ⇒ zero-shot transfer" is a general principle or an ESM2-specific accident.

Added value to my researchThis is the design I would carry straight into GeoAI. Today geospatial models tokenize land use and POIs by categorical codes, which is precisely why they do not cross national data standards. Replacing those codes with embeddings from a universal descriptor space (text descriptions, imagery, or functional signatures) should make a spatial foundation model portable across countries with incompatible POI taxonomies — the geospatial equivalent of dropping the orthologue table. That is my most direct path to fine-tuning geospatial foundation models for cross-national urban-health and accessibility work.
