The Virtual Tissues foundation model resolves spatial proteomics across scales

Johann Wenckstern · Eeshaan Jain · Benedikt von Querfurth · Yexiang Cheng · Kiril Vasilev · Matteo Pariset · Phil F. Cheng · Petros Liakopoulos · Olivier Michielin · Andreas Wicki · Gabriele Gut · Charlotte Bunne Published 2026-08-05 DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10884-y Source nature.com ↗
Core Conclusion

VirTues is a marker-aware foundation model that turns fragmented multiplex tissue-imaging cohorts into one queryable "virtual tissue" atlas. By embedding every protein marker through a protein language model (ESM-2) and factorizing transformer attention into marker and spatial components, a single pretrained backbone serves marker inpainting, cell segmentation and typing, niche annotation, patient retrieval and biomarker discovery — including zero-shot transfer to marker panels and imaging technologies never seen in training. In triple-negative breast cancer its learned spatial signatures predict anti-PD-L1 chemo-immunotherapy response (AUROC 0.823, +5.14% over the best published spatial predictor on the same cohort) and stratify disease-free survival in an independent cohort (c-index 0.628 vs 0.606), beating single-marker and immune-ratio schemes.

★

Why It Matters

  • It solves the field's structural problem, not just a benchmark. Every spatial-proteomics study uses its own antibody panel, platform and protocol, so models were locked to single cohorts. Representing a marker by its protein sequence places heterogeneous panels in one shared space — heterogeneity becomes a feature of the input, not a barrier.
  • One frozen backbone, the whole workflow. Segmentation, cell typing, niche annotation, virtual staining of unmeasured markers, tissue-level prediction and case retrieval all read from the same encoder. That collapses a chain of bespoke, separately tuned tools into one reusable computational layer.
  • A new generation of biomarkers. Instead of one marker or one cell-type frequency, the signatures are multi-cellular spatial programmes (e.g. apoptotic + DNA-damaged + PD-L1+GZMB+ cells with a specific neighbourhood composition). Crucially, raw mean marker intensities did not resolve the responder/non-responder difference — the signal exists only in the learned representation.
  • Externally validated, and it moves the clinical needle. Signatures discovered in NeoTRIP transferred fully zero-shot to an independent TNBC cohort and outperformed both the published biomarker system and current clinical stratification — the rarest and most convincing result in this literature.
  • Open infrastructure. The harmonized corpus (the largest open collection of spatial proteomics assembled to date), the code and the pretrained weights are released, so the atlas is extensible by others rather than a one-off artefact.

Core Methods

  • Marker-aware tokenization: each 128 × 128 crop becomes a 3-D grid of image tokens (marker × position); an ESM-2 protein embedding of the marker is linearly projected and added to each image token, so the model knows what molecule each channel is — the mechanism that lets unseen markers be encoded without retraining.
  • Factorized sparse attention: the ViT encoder restricts direct token interaction to either the same position across channels (marker attention) or the same channel across positions (spatial attention), breaking the quadratic blow-up in channel × spatial dimensions and yielding attention maps that are separately interpretable over markers and regions.
  • Masked autoencoding with three masking regimes: independent masking (channel-wise random ratio 60–100%), marker masking (an entire marker hidden → virtual staining), and niche masking (all markers hidden at a set of positions → spatial inpainting). Average reconstruction under independent masking: Pearson r = 0.800 ± 0.130.
  • Hierarchical summary tokens: learnable patch summary tokens are convolved with the cell segmentation mask to give cell tokens, and aggregated into niche and tissue tokens — one encoder, four analysis scales.
  • Downstream heads on a frozen backbone: logistic regression / linear probing for cell typing; a joint instance + semantic segmentation head on patch representations (benchmarked against Cellpose, InstanSeg, StarDist, MAPS, Astir); ABMIL attention-based multiple-instance learning for tissue-level clinical prediction.
  • Patient retrieval by optimal transport: niche summary tokens form a searchable database; similarity uses OT / sliced Wasserstein distance, evaluated by top-3 precision for subtype, grade, nodal metastasis and relapse against KRONOS, CA-MAE and ResNet baselines.
  • Biomarker discovery pipeline (unsupervised → supervised): embed pre-treatment cells → multi-resolution Leiden clustering (r ∈ [4, 5], Δr = 0.05) → per-patient cluster frequencies → rank clusters by out-of-fold AUROC and response risk → retain 2 response + 2 non-response signatures → multivariate model (AUROC 0.823) → random-forest transfer to an independent cohort.
  • Statistics: Kaplan–Meier with log-rank tests, Wald and bootstrap 95% CIs (n = 1,000 resamples), two-sided McNemar tests for retrieval, two-sided proportion z-tests for neighbourhood enrichment, concordance index for survival stratification.

Expanded Ideas — this paper's extensibility & added value to my research

Each idea stays anchored to this paper: first how the paper itself could be extended (drawing on our corpus), then the added value for my research lines (migis.net — GeoAI, health geography & 2SFCA accessibility, explainable and physics-informed spatial learning, geospatial foundation-model fine-tuning).

① Marker tokens as a general "variable-panel interface" — and a panel-design calculator

Extending this paperVirTues' decisive trick is that a channel is described semantically (its ESM-2 protein embedding) rather than by its index, so unseen panels land in the same space. But the paper conditions only on marker identity, not on how the marker was measured — platform, antibody clone, dilution, scanner, batch. A direct extension: add protocol tokens alongside marker tokens, so the encoder can separate "what molecule" from "how it was assayed," and then exploit the geometry the paper already visualizes (Fig. 2f: UMAP distance from an unseen marker to its nearest pretrained neighbours) as a prospective predictor of zero-shot fidelity. That turns a descriptive diagnostic into a panel-design calculator: before a lab spends money on antibodies, the model tells them which markers it can already virtually stain and which would add genuinely new information.

Added value to my researchThis is the missing primitive for cross-city geospatial models. My spatial-accessibility and health-geography models break at city and country borders precisely because the variable panel changes: census schemas differ, POI taxonomies differ, mobile-phone providers differ. Adopting VirTues' design — embed each covariate through a text/ontology embedding of its definition (an "OSM-tag language model" or a census-codebook encoder) and add it to the raster token — would let one model pretrained on Chinese and US urban data ingest a new country's idiosyncratic variable set without schema harmonization. The panel-design calculator becomes a data-acquisition calculator: which additional data layer actually buys predictive power for a given city, and which is already implied by what I have.

② Factorized marker × spatial attention as an interpretable spatially varying operator

Extending this paperThe paper's factorization is not only a compute trick — it produces two separately readable attention channels ("attention pinpoints markers and regions"), which is unusually clean for a ViT. Yet the attributions remain qualitative. Extending it: turn marker attention into a calibrated, statistically tested attribution — permutation nulls per marker, stability across bootstrap resamples, and a check of whether attention-ranked markers agree with the ablation-based ground truth of removing that channel. Then compare the spatial attention field against classical spatial statistics on the same tissue, since what the model is implicitly estimating is a local, context-dependent weighting of neighbours — a learned, anisotropic kernel rather than a fixed radius.

Added value to my researchThis maps almost one-to-one onto my MGWR / explainable-spatial-ML agenda. MGWR gives every location a coefficient per covariate with a bandwidth chosen by cross-validation; VirTues' factorized attention gives per-location, per-variable weights learned end-to-end. Building a "factorized-attention GWR" for urban tensors (grid × {POI density, land use, mobility, greenness, deprivation}) would give me local coefficient surfaces with the expressiveness of a transformer and, unlike a black-box CNN, an interpretable variable axis — plus a principled way to report which covariate mattered where, which is what health-policy audiences actually ask for.

③ Optimal-transport case retrieval → case-based reasoning for places

Extending this paperFigure 4g–k is quietly the most transferable contribution: patients are compared as distributions of niche tokens under a sliced-Wasserstein metric, not as averaged feature vectors — which is why retrieval recovers molecular and architectural similarity. Two extensions the paper leaves open: (a) make retrieval outcome-conditioned — retrieve the most similar patients who received a different treatment, converting the index into a nearest-neighbour counterfactual engine for decision support; and (b) attach calibrated uncertainty to each retrieval, since a nearest neighbour in a sparse region of the atlas is not evidence. Scaling the index from one cohort to the full 32-cohort corpus is the natural stress test.

Added value to my researchReplace "patient" with "neighbourhood" and this is a policy-transfer engine for health geography. Comparing census tracts by their distribution of local features — POI mixture, mobility-flow profile, primary-care travel-time distribution, sociodemographic composition — under an OT metric is strictly more faithful than the tract-mean vectors that most similarity analyses use, because two tracts with identical means can have completely different internal structure. Concretely: given an underserved tract, retrieve the most structurally similar tracts that already received a telehealth or clinic-siting intervention, and read off the realized effect. That is an evidence-transfer tool for the digital-divide and 2SFCA work, and it degrades gracefully where causal identification is impossible.

④ Stress-test the zero-shot claim: site/technology shift, and who pays for it

Extending this paperThe paper demonstrates transfer across panels and across four technologies — but the confounding structure of that transfer is untested. In multi-cohort imaging, cohort identity is entangled with hospital, scanner, staining protocol and patient population, so a model that appears to generalize may be exploiting site-specific shortcuts. Extending it: a causal audit that separates acquisition shift (platform, batch) from population shift (age, stage, ancestry, treatment era), leave-one-site-out rather than leave-one-dataset-out evaluation, and — critically — subgroup performance reporting for the biomarkers. A signature with AUROC 0.823 overall can still be near-random in an under-represented subgroup, and a Nature-grade clinical claim should say which.

Added value to my researchIdentical failure mode, different substrate. My multi-source fusion work (mobile-phone records, social media, remote sensing) suffers exactly this entanglement: platform penetration correlates with age and income, so a model "generalizing" to a new city may be tracking who is observable rather than who is there. Importing this audit protocol — explicit acquisition-vs-population shift decomposition, leave-one-city-out, mandatory subgroup breakdowns for accessibility and telehealth-equity metrics — would materially harden my equity conclusions, which is where reviewers of health-policy work press hardest.

⑤ The scale ladder (patch → cell → niche → tissue) is a MAUP problem in disguise

Extending this paperVirTues aggregates patch tokens into niche and tissue tokens, but the niche is defined by an aggregation choice, and the paper does not report how sensitive its risk groups and biomarkers are to that choice. This is the modifiable areal unit problem in tissue: change the niche radius or the clustering resolution and the discovered signature may move. Extending it: a systematic scale-sensitivity analysis — sweep niche size and Leiden resolution jointly, report which signatures are scale-invariant, and treat scale stability as a selection criterion for biomarkers rather than an afterthought. Better still, let the model learn a scale-adaptive niche supervised by outcome, so the aggregation unit is discovered rather than fixed. The unsupervised-then-supervised discovery pipeline (embed → multi-resolution cluster → rank by out-of-fold AUROC → external transfer) is itself a template worth stating as a general protocol.

Added value to my researchMAUP is the oldest unsolved problem in my field and the paper hands me a fresh operationalization: a signature is only real if it survives its scale sweep. I can lift the whole pipeline for neighbourhood-level health analysis — embed grid cells with a geospatial foundation model, cluster at multiple resolutions, rank clusters by out-of-fold prediction of a health outcome (mental-health service use, avoidable admissions), keep only scale-stable clusters, then transfer them zero-shot to another city and validate. That yields "urban signatures" that are multi-feature and multi-scale rather than single indicators like density or NDVI — a direct upgrade to my spatiotemporal KDE and healthy-cities work, and a defensible answer to the standard "your result is an artefact of your spatial unit" objection.
