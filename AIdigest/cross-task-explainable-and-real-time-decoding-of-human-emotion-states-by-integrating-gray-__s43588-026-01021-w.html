Cross-task, explainable and real-time decoding of human emotion states by integrating gray and white matter intracranial neural activity

Yuxiao Yang · Wenjun Chen · Yuyang Chen · Ling Ding · Chi Zhang · Hongjie Jiang · Zhoule Zhu · Xinxia Guo · Shuang Wang · Gang Pan · Ning Wei · Shaohua Hu · Junming Zhu · Yueming Wang Published 2026-07-22 DOI 10.1038/s43588-026-01021-w Source nature.com ↗
Core Conclusion

Personalized deep-learning decoders trained on intracranial EEG can track continuous valence and arousal at a performance level above prior EEG and iEEG work — and the decisive gain comes from integrating white matter signals with gray matter signals, a source most iEEG studies discard as noise. The models generalize across two very different emotion-eliciting tasks (video and image), expose shared and preference-specific mesolimbic–thalamo–cortical subnetworks that make the decoding neurophysiologically explainable, and run robustly in real time in four new individuals. Together this moves affective brain–computer interfaces from offline proof-of-concept toward deployable, closed-loop therapeutic systems.

Why It Matters

  • Rehabilitates white matter as signal, not artifact. Stereo-EEG contacts that land in white matter are routinely excluded from analysis. This paper shows that adding them substantially improves decoding of valence and arousal — a data-efficiency result that instantly increases the usable channel yield of every existing clinical iEEG archive.
  • Attacks the four blockers at once. Prior emotion decoding work has typically delivered one of {high accuracy, cross-context generalization, explainability, real-time operation}. This study is designed to deliver all four in the same cohort — which is what "deployable" actually requires.
  • Continuous states, not discrete emotion labels. Decoding fine-grained continuous valence/arousal ratings (Russell's circumplex axes) rather than n-way emotion categories is the representation a closed-loop therapy controller can actually act on.
  • Cross-task generalization is the honest stress test. A model trained during a video task and evaluated on an image task (and vice versa) tests whether the decoder learned emotion or learned the stimulus paradigm — the same confound that defeats most lab-to-life transfer.
  • Clinical translation target is explicit. The stated destination is closed-loop neuromodulation for affective disorders (treatment-resistant depression, PTSD), where a validated, real-time affect readout is the missing feedback signal.
  • Explainability is anatomical, not just numerical. The models resolve which gray-matter regions and which white-matter tracts carry valence versus arousal, converting a black-box decoder into a statement about circuit organization.

Core Methods

  • Simultaneous neural + behavioural acquisition: iEEG recorded while participants self-rate valence and arousal continuously across two distinct emotion-eliciting paradigms (affective videos; affective images), giving paired neural/label time series rather than block-averaged categories.
  • Personalized deep-learning decoders: a per-individual model within a shared deep learning framework maps multi-band spectro-spatial iEEG features to continuous valence and arousal, avoiding the electrode-correspondence problem that blocks naive cross-subject pooling.
  • Gray + white matter integration: the central ablation — decoding from gray matter alone, white matter alone, and both — quantifies the added information carried by white-matter contacts (Fig. 4, Extended Data Fig. 5d–f).
  • Interchangeable decoding: an intra-task model, an inter-task (trained on the other task) model and a random model are all evaluated on the same target-task test data — an apples-to-apples generalization test (Fig. 3, Extended Data Fig. 4a–d).
  • Transferable decoding: a source-task-pretrained model and a randomly initialized model are fine-tuned with varying amounts of target-task data, tracing a data-efficiency curve for transfer (Fig. 3, Extended Data Fig. 4e–h).
  • Encoding explainability: region- and tract-level contribution analysis identifies shared versus valence- or arousal-preferring nodes of a mesolimbic–thalamo–cortical subnetwork; spectral and temporal interpretation isolates band-specific (γ-dominant) and time-locked contributions (Fig. 4, Extended Data Fig. 5).
  • Temporal structure of decoded states: frequency-domain power density of the decoded valence/arousal time series is compared against a random-decoding null to identify the characteristic timescale of the decoded affect signal.
  • Real-time online system: the trained decoder is deployed in a streaming pipeline (Qt6/FFmpeg-supported acquisition and presentation) and run prospectively in four new individuals, with a supplementary video demonstrating live decoding (Fig. 5).
  • Statistics: two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with FDR correction throughout; permutation/random-model baselines define chance; t-SNE and clustering-validity indices used for latent structure.

Expanded Ideas — this paper's extensibility & added value to my research

① From 18 personalized decoders to one population-level, anatomy-tokenized affect foundation model

Extending this paperThe paper's decoders are strictly personalized: each of the 18 individuals gets their own model, because electrode montages differ from patient to patient and there is no common channel space. That design is what forces every new patient to supply their own labeled ratings before the system works — the single biggest barrier to clinical deployment. The extension is to stop treating the electrode index as the token and start treating the anatomical coordinate as the token: encode each contact by its MNI position plus its FreeSurfer/DKT gray-matter label or its white-matter tract identity, and pretrain a shared encoder across all 22 individuals (18 offline + 4 online) in that anatomy-indexed space, with self-supervised masked-spectrogram objectives on the vast amount of unlabeled clinical iEEG that every epilepsy monitoring unit already stores. A new patient then arrives with a prior over how their montage maps into affect-encoding subnetworks, and the paper's own fine-tuning curve (Fig. 3, Extended Data Fig. 4f–h) becomes the natural evaluation axis — how few target ratings are now needed to reach the personalized ceiling. The paper's white-matter result makes this especially attractive, since tracts are far more consistent across brains than individual gray-matter contact placements, giving the shared model an anatomically stable backbone to align on.

Added value to my researchThis is structurally identical to the problem I face fine-tuning geospatial foundation models: every city has its own "montage" (different POI taxonomies, different census geographies, different sensor coverage), so models are rebuilt per city and never accumulate. The anatomy-as-token trick translates directly to geography-as-token — encode each observation by its position in a canonical spatial embedding (H3 cell, functional-region label, network centrality signature) rather than by its local administrative ID, pretrain across cities, and then fine-tune with the handful of labels a new city can afford. The fine-tuning curve is exactly the deliverable my collaborators want: "how many surveyed neighborhoods do we need before the transferred mental-health accessibility model beats a locally trained one?"

② Make the mesolimbic–thalamo–cortical subnetwork architectural: a tract-constrained, physics-informed graph decoder

Extending this paperThe paper's explainability is read out after the fact: it trains a decoder, then attributes performance back to gray-matter regions and white-matter tracts to reveal shared versus valence-/arousal-preferring subnetworks (Fig. 4). But the discovered structure is never fed back into the model. The extension is to close that loop — build the decoder as a graph neural network whose nodes are anatomically labeled contacts and whose edges are the subject's own white-matter tracts, with edge weights initialized from HCP-derived connectivity and a conduction-delay term on each edge so that signal propagation obeys a physical transmission prior rather than free attention. Two things follow that the current design cannot give. First, explainability becomes intrinsic (which edges carry affect information is a learned parameter you can read directly) rather than a post-hoc attribution that the interpretability literature warns can be unfaithful in high-stakes settings. Second, the white-matter contacts stop being extra channels and become what the anatomy says they are — measurements taken on the wires, midway along a tract, which is a far stronger inductive bias than treating them as additional independent sensors. A sparsity penalty on edges would also test whether the affect code is genuinely a compact subnetwork or a superposition spread across many overlapping circuits.

Added value to my researchThis is my physics-informed graph learning agenda in a different substrate, and the analogy is unusually tight: white-matter tracts are to gray-matter regions what road/transit networks are to activity centers — conduits with finite propagation speed, capacity, and measurable mid-link state. A decoder that reads "signals on the wires" is exactly a model that reads traffic mid-link rather than only at origins and destinations. I can lift the tract-constrained GNN formulation wholesale into job–housing flow modeling and healthcare service-area delineation, where edge-level (road-segment) observations are usually discarded in favour of zone-level aggregates. It also sharpens my explainable-spatial-ML line: intrinsic edge importance is defensible to a health-department audience in a way that post-hoc SHAP maps on a black box never quite are.

③ Add a third "task": the real environment — pair the decoder with where and when the person actually is

Extending this paperThe paper's two contexts are both screen-delivered laboratory paradigms — affective videos and affective images — and its generalization claim is video↔image. That is a real test of stimulus-modality invariance, but both tasks share the same passive, seated, bedside setting. The honest next context is the one the clinical destination requires: free behavior in the ward. Epilepsy monitoring patients are already continuously recorded for days, with video, and are already sampled for mood by clinical instruments. Extending the paper means running its decoder over that unstructured recording and validating decoded valence/arousal against (a) periodic ecological momentary assessment prompts, (b) ward-context annotations — visitor present, window light, time of day, meal, procedure — and (c) the temporal-structure analysis it already performs (the characteristic timescale of decoded affect, Extended Data Fig. 5g–h), which should shift when the driver is life rather than a 20-second clip. This converts a stimulus-locked decoder into an ambulatory affect monitor and, critically, tests whether the mesolimbic–thalamo–cortical code it identified is task-evoked or state-general.

Added value to my researchThis is the missing gold standard for my environment→mental-health work. Every greenness-dose and urban-nature study I build on infers affect from self-report or survey scales, which are coarse, recall-biased, and inconsistently scaled between cohorts. A neurally-decoded continuous valence/arousal signal — even from a tiny, clinically atypical sample — provides a physiological anchor against which to calibrate the cheap, scalable proxies I actually deploy at population scale (social-media sentiment, mobility-derived activity spaces, survey instruments). Concretely: co-register decoded affect with ward-level environmental context to estimate a neural-to-self-report transfer function, then use that function to reweight the exposure–response curves in my greenspace and urbanicity models. It also gives me a principled way to argue about temporal resolution — my spatiotemporal KDE and exposure models assume affect responds on some timescale, and this paper actually measures one.

④ Turn cross-task transfer from an empirical finding into a predictable quantity: a decodable-shift index

Extending this paperFigure 3 and Extended Data Fig. 4 establish that transfer works and how much target data it saves, in both directions (video→image and image→video). What they do not provide is a way to predict transfer success before spending the data — which is precisely what a clinician deploying this system needs when the target "task" is a new patient, a new stimulus set, or next month's recording session after electrode drift and medication changes. The extension is to build a conditional-shift diagnostic on top of the paper's own machinery: measure the divergence between source and target in the decoder's latent space (the t-SNE embedding it already computes, made quantitative), and in the label distribution of valence/arousal ratings, then regress the observed transfer gap against those divergences across all directed source→target pairs the cohort affords. The result is a shift index that says in advance "this transfer will cost you 40% of your target labels" — and the operator-learning-under-conditional-shift literature supplies the formal setting for exactly this kind of source→target map. As a bonus, the same index flags when a deployed decoder has drifted far enough to need recalibration, which any chronic implant will eventually do.

Added value to my research"Will my model transfer to your city?" is the single most common question I get, and I currently answer it by trying. A decodable-shift index gives me an a priori transferability score built from covariate divergence plus response-distribution divergence between a source and target region — directly usable for cross-city transfer of accessibility, telehealth-adoption, and EV-siting models. It also reframes something I already study: MGWR's spatial non-stationarity is domain shift measured locally. If the bandwidth surface and the shift index are two views of the same object, then I can use the transfer-cost curve to choose where a globally pretrained GeoAI model may be applied as-is versus where local refitting is mandatory — turning a methodological nuisance into a deployment map.

⑤ Close the loop responsibly: from an open-loop readout to a controller with uncertainty, and up to the service geography around the patient

Extending this paperThe real-time demonstration in four new individuals (Fig. 5) is a genuine systems milestone, but it is still open loop: the decoder reports, nothing acts. Two extensions are needed before it can drive therapy. First, the decoder must emit calibrated uncertainty and an abstain option — a closed-loop stimulator that fires on a confidently wrong valence estimate is worse than one that does nothing, so the model needs to know when the montage is noisy, the patient is asleep, or the state is off-manifold. Second, the target should be the affect state's dynamics, not its instantaneous value: work showing that affective states are maintained by slow attractor dynamics implies a controller should be steering the system's trajectory and persistence, which pairs naturally with this paper's frequency-domain analysis of the decoded time series. A model-predictive controller built on decoded valence/arousal, with tract-level stimulation targets taken from the paper's own explainability map (Fig. 4), would be the first affect BCI whose stimulation site is chosen by the decoder's attribution rather than by anatomical convention. Then, beyond the implant: a decoded affect trajectory is a longitudinal clinical signal, and its value multiplies when linked to what care the patient can actually reach after discharge.

Added value to my researchTwo of my lines meet here. Methodologically, "streaming state estimation with calibrated uncertainty feeding a control policy" is the architecture I want for online spatiotemporal KDE and real-time service allocation — my EV-charging efficiency-versus-equity optimizer is currently a batch solve, and the abstain/uncertainty machinery is what would make a live version safe to act on. Substantively, this paper defines the ideal individual-level mental-health signal, and my work defines whether the corresponding care is spatially reachable: decoded deterioration is only actionable if a psychiatric provider, crisis responder, or telehealth channel is within a viable catchment. That is a concrete joint study — combine a validated affect trajectory with 2SFCA/R2SFCA mental-health accessibility and telehealth-divide measures to ask where a closed-loop or remote-monitoring intervention would actually change outcomes, and where the bottleneck is geography rather than neurotechnology.
