The traditional Chinese medicine Lianhua Qingwen in preventing secondary household transmission of influenza: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial

Yangqing Zhan · Zhengshi Lin · Zhonghao Fang · Benjamin J. Cowling · Nancy H. L. Leung · … · Nanshan Zhong · Zifeng Yang (27 authors) Published 2026-06-26 DOI 10.1038/s44360-026-00149-3 Source nature.com ↗
Core Conclusion

In a 55-centre, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in China (Nov 2023 – Mar 2024), 5 days of Lianhua Qingwen (LHQW) given to influenza-negative household contacts cut secondary infection by roughly two-thirds: 53/728 (7.28%) in the LHQW arm versus 104/700 (14.86%) on placebo (odds ratio 0.33; 95% CI 0.17–0.61; P < 0.001). The effect was consistent across prespecified subgroups (age, sex, antiviral use in the index case, symptom-onset timing, high-risk contacts), and adverse events were infrequent and comparable between arms with no severe events. The claim is therefore that a cheap, over-the-counter herbal formulation can serve as post-exposure prophylaxis where prescription antivirals are limited by cost, access, or resistance — a result whose main caveat is that the study drug's manufacturer funded the trial and participated in design, data collection and data review.

Why It Matters

  • Households are the engine room of seasonal influenza. Most onward transmission happens in the home, yet post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is rarely deployed at scale because oseltamivir, zanamivir, laninamivir and baloxavir all require a prescription, a clinic visit and a supply chain. This trial tests a product already sitting on pharmacy shelves.
  • It converts an accessibility problem into a pharmacological one — and back. An OTC prophylactic changes who can be protected: the binding constraint moves from prescriber availability to retail proximity, which is a geographic question rather than a clinical one.
  • Effect size is large and the safety signal is quiet. An OR of 0.33 with no severe adverse events is, on its face, competitive with the neuraminidase-inhibitor PEP literature the paper itself tabulates (Extended Data Table 3).
  • Rigor is unusual for herbal medicine. Double-blind, placebo-controlled, 55 centres, prospectively registered (ChiCTR2300074385), with serial RT-PCR rather than symptom-based endpoints — this is the design standard TCM efficacy claims are usually faulted for lacking.
  • The conflict of interest is the story's other half. Funding, drug supply, and involvement in design/collection/review by Shijiazhuang Yiling Pharmaceutical means independent replication — ideally outside China and outside the sponsor's network — is the decisive next experiment, not an optional one.

Core Methods

  • Index-then-contact recruitment. Enrol a laboratory-confirmed influenza index case, then enrol influenza-negative household/residential close contacts of that case — the classical household-PEP design that makes the exposure clock start at a known point.
  • 1:1 double-blind randomization with matched placebo, with allocation at the contact level and analysis across three prespecified populations (full analysis set, per-protocol set, intention-to-treat set) to guard against per-protocol optimism.
  • Virological endpoint via serial RT-PCR over 9 days rather than clinician-diagnosed illness — this captures subclinical secondary infection that symptom-based trials systematically miss, and it is the reason the placebo-arm SIR (14.86%) is credible.
  • Fixed short-course dosing: 4 × 0.35 g capsules t.i.d. for 5 days, i.e. prophylaxis is compressed into the window in which household transmission is known to peak.
  • Odds-ratio estimation with 95% CI plus prespecified subgroup analysis across age, sex, antiviral use in the index case, timing of index symptom onset, and high-risk contact status (Extended Data Table 8) — the paper's evidence that the effect is not carried by one stratum.
  • Symptomatic and time-resolved secondary outcomes (Fig. 3, Extended Data Tables 2 and 4) evaluated at multiple post-initiation time points, using a structured Influenza Symptom Scorecard.
  • Explicit benchmarking against the published neuraminidase-inhibitor and cap-dependent-endonuclease PEP trials (Extended Data Table 3), which is how the paper argues clinical relevance rather than mere statistical significance.

① Geocode the 55 centres: turn a trial into a spatial external-validity map

Extending this paperThe trial's single most under-exploited asset is that it ran at 55 centres spanning coastal megacities (Guangzhou, Beijing), inland provincial capitals (Wuhan, Chengdu, Xi'an/Xianyang) and smaller prefecture cities (Liuzhou, Linhai) — yet the published record reports one pooled odds ratio and no site-level geography. Extending the paper: release centre-level SIR with coordinates and fit a multilevel / geographically weighted logistic model in which the treatment effect is allowed to vary by site context (household size, housing density, heating regime, baseline antiviral prescribing rate, local epidemic phase at enrolment). That converts "the trial met its primary outcome" into "here is the map of where the 0.33 holds and where the CI opens up" — the difference between an efficacy claim and a deployability claim, and the honest way to answer the sponsor-funding critique without a new trial.

Added value to my researchThis is exactly the MGWR-on-clinical-outcomes template I want for health geography: a randomized treatment effect as the dependent variable and built-environment/service-supply covariates as spatially varying predictors. It gives me a defensible causal anchor (randomization removes confounding within site) while letting spatial non-stationarity do the explanatory work — a much stronger design than the observational spatial-health regressions I usually run, and directly transferable to my telehealth-uptake and primary-care-access models.

② Push the per-contact odds ratio into a household-structured transmission model

Extending this paperThe trial estimates protection for an individual randomized contact, but households are not independent units: if several contacts in one home are protected, the household's internal transmission chain is truncated and the benefit compounds nonlinearly. Extending the paper: embed the measured OR of 0.33 as a per-exposure susceptibility multiplier in a household-structured SIR / agent-based model calibrated to Chinese household size distributions, then ask the questions the trial cannot: what community-level attack-rate reduction follows from 20% vs 60% household PEP uptake; what fraction of the benefit is direct versus indirect protection; and at what uptake threshold OTC prophylaxis starts bending the epidemic curve rather than merely protecting purchasers. The trial's 9-day window also cannot see rebound — a model can test whether delayed infection simply shifts cases later in the season.

Added value to my researchThis is my agent-based-modelling and Monte-Carlo line applied to a rare luxury: a randomized, PCR-confirmed parameter instead of a literature-scraped prior. It also gives me a clean way to demonstrate the direct-vs-indirect decomposition machinery I need for intervention-siting work, where the whole argument is that placing a resource somewhere protects people who never touch it.

③ The presymptomatic window: prophylaxis timing as the hidden effect modifier

Extending this paperThe trial prespecifies "symptom-onset timing" as a subgroup but treats it as a categorical covariate. The transmission literature says the clock is the mechanism: influenza A shows substantial presymptomatic household transmission, so by the time an index case is laboratory-confirmed and their contacts are enrolled, a share of those contacts are already infected but PCR-negative. Extending the paper: re-analyse with time-since-index-onset as a continuous exposure, model contact-level serial RT-PCR as a survival/viral-trajectory problem rather than a binary endpoint, and stratify by influenza A versus B — a formulation that could show LHQW's apparent efficacy is partly a race against an already-started clock, and would tell public-health services exactly how many hours of enrolment delay dissolve the benefit. That is the most decision-relevant re-analysis available from data the investigators already hold.

Added value to my researchSquarely my spatiotemporal kernel-density and time-to-event toolkit: exposure timing plus contact location gives a space–time intensity surface where the treatment effect decays along both axes. It also sharpens a question I keep hitting in accessibility work — that "access" is not a distance but a latency budget, and the value of a nearby pharmacy is entirely a function of how many hours it saves.

④ Who can actually reach the pill? An accessibility audit of OTC prophylaxis

Extending this paperThe paper's central policy argument is accessibility — LHQW is OTC where oseltamivir needs a prescription — but the trial never measures accessibility at all. Extending it: run a 2SFCA/R2SFCA audit of the actual supply geography, comparing the catchment population within a 15-minute walk of a pharmacy stocking LHQW against the population within reach of a clinic that can prescribe an antiviral, across the same provinces the 55 centres sit in. The output — a per-population "PEP reachability" ratio — is what turns the trial's OR into a population-level averted-infection estimate and tests whether the OTC advantage is real everywhere or only in dense urban cores. It also names the failure mode the trial cannot see: an efficacious drug that nobody can buy in the window when it works.

Added value to my researchThis is my accessibility line with a new and unusually crisp outcome. Most 2SFCA studies stop at a supply–demand ratio because no one can price the health value of proximity; here the efficacy per unit of timely access is randomized and known, so the accessibility surface can be converted into expected infections averted. ⑤ From one season to a targeting engine: mobility-informed forecasting meets household PEP

Extending this paperThe trial covers a single season (Nov 2023 – Mar 2024) in one country, so its generalizability across subtype composition, epidemic phase and mobility regime is untested. Extending the paper: couple the household-PEP effect with mobility-driven influenza forecasting so that prophylaxis becomes a targeted rather than a uniform intervention — pre-position OTC stock and issue household-PEP advisories in the prefectures where a mobility-informed model predicts the epidemic wavefront will arrive in the next 1–2 weeks. Pair this with an explainable-ML heterogeneous-treatment-effect analysis (causal forest on the trial's covariates) to learn which contact profiles carry the benefit, and the result is a targeting rule instead of a blanket recommendation. It would also stress-test whether the pooled OR survives outside the specific 2023/24 subtype mix.
