Bridging three-dimensional molecular structures and artificial intelligence with a conformation description language

Jiacheng Xiong · Yuqi Shi · Min Wu · Panpan Shao · Zhaokun Wang · Wei Zhang · Runze Zhang · Zhiyi Chen · Chuanlong Zeng · Xun Jiang · Duanhua Cao · Zhaoping Xiong · Zunyun Fu · Sulin Zhang · Mingyue Zheng Published 2026-06-11 DOI 10.1038/s42256-026-01250-8 Source nature.com ↗
Core Conclusion

ConfSeq is a conformation description language that turns a 3D molecular structure into a discrete token sequence: it splices SMILES with internal coordinates — dihedral angles, bond angles and a pseudo-chirality descriptor — so the representation is SE(3)-invariant by construction while staying as compact and human-readable as SMILES. Because 3D geometry becomes text, three previously separate problems — conformation prediction, de novo 3D generation, and 3D representation learning — collapse into ordinary sequence modelling, and a plain transformer (no equivariant architecture, no diffusion) reaches state-of-the-art across the benchmarks tested. The framework was pushed all the way to wet-lab validation, yielding novel STING and ALDH1B1 inhibitors with IC50 values of 0.338–3.51 μM.

Why It Matters

  • It removes the architectural tax on 3D learning. For years, respecting rotation/translation symmetry meant building it into the network (equivariant GNNs, torsional diffusion, geometric deep learning). ConfSeq moves the symmetry into the representation: internal coordinates are invariant, so any off-the-shelf transformer inherits SE(3) invariance for free.
  • It unlocks the LLM stack for 3D chemistry. Once conformations are tokens, everything the language-model ecosystem has built — pretraining, scaling, instruction tuning, in-context learning, shared tokenizer vocabularies with text — becomes directly applicable to 3D molecular modelling, which the abstract identifies as the missing piece.
  • One representation, three tasks. Conformation prediction, unconditional/shape-conditioned generation, and representation learning are normally three separate model families with three sets of tricks. Unifying them as next-token prediction is a genuine simplification, not just a re-skin.
  • It survives contact with reality. The paper does not stop at benchmark tables: ConfSeq-driven virtual screening produced experimentally confirmed STING and ALDH1B1 inhibitors in the low-micromolar range (0.338–3.51 μM), with western-blot source data provided for Fig. 6.
  • Readability is a real scientific asset. SMILES won partly because a chemist can read it. ConfSeq preserves that property for 3D — a rare case where a learned-system input is also a human-auditable object.

Core Methods

  • Internal-coordinate tokenization (the central idea). Instead of Cartesian atom positions, the conformation is described by dihedral angles, bond angles and a pseudo-chirality descriptor woven into the SMILES string. Internal coordinates are unchanged by rotation and translation, so SE(3) invariance is a property of the encoding, not of the network.
  • Pseudo-chirality descriptor. Internal coordinates alone cannot distinguish mirror images; the added descriptor restores the handedness information that a purely angular encoding would lose — the piece that makes the language faithful rather than merely convenient.
  • Discretization of continuous geometry. Angles must become a finite vocabulary for token-based modelling. This is the representation's core trade-off (resolution vs. sequence/vocabulary size); the exact binning scheme is specified in the paywalled Methods.
  • Task unification as sequence modelling. Conformation prediction becomes conditional generation (2D graph → 3D token string); de novo design becomes unconditional generation; representation learning becomes encoding the same token stream — all with standard transformer architectures, deliberately avoiding equivariant GNNs and diffusion samplers.
  • Shape-conditioned generation. Generation conditioned on a target molecular shape, evaluated against shape-conditioned diffusion baselines (DiffSMol) and equivariant shape-conditioned generation (Adams & Coley, ICLR 2023).
  • Evaluation suite. Distributional metrics for generation (Fréchet ChemNet Distance), physical-plausibility checking in the spirit of PoseBusters, 3D similarity/alignment tools (LS-align, SHAFTS, ShaEP) for screening, and distance correlation (Székely et al., 2007) for representation analysis.
  • Prospective validation. ConfSeq-driven ligand-based screening → compound selection → biochemical/cellular assays for STING and ALDH1B1, with western blots supporting the STING mechanism (Fig. 6).

Expanded Ideas — this paper's extensibility & added value to my research

Each idea stays anchored to this paper: first how ConfSeq itself could be extended (drawing on our corpus), then the added value for my research lines (migis.net — GeoAI, spatiotemporal analysis, 2SFCA/R2SFCA accessibility, explainable & physics-informed graph learning, geospatial foundation models). The transferable asset here is not chemistry — it is the recipe for turning continuous, symmetry-constrained geometry into a readable token language.

① Scale the description language past small molecules — periodic, macromolecular and complexed systems

Extending this paperConfSeq is demonstrated on single, isolated, drug-like molecules. Its internal-coordinate trick has a known ceiling: dihedral chains grow long and error compounds along the chain for large or ring-dense systems, and the language has no vocabulary for a second body — a protein pocket, a crystal lattice, a solvent shell. The obvious next paper is a hierarchical ConfSeq: fragment-level anchor tokens carrying local frames, with intra-fragment torsions beneath them, plus explicit inter-molecular relative-pose tokens (a 6-DoF relative frame expressed invariantly) so protein–ligand complexes, co-crystal packing and periodic materials all become one grammar. That would let a single transformer do docking, co-folding and conformer generation in the same token stream instead of the paper's ligand-only scope. Concretely testable: does hierarchical tokenization stop the error accumulation that flat torsion chains suffer on macrocycles?

Added value to my researchThis is exactly the design I need for an urban 3D description language. Building footprints, heights, street-network geometry and terrain are continuous, translation/rotation-arbitrary, and hierarchical — block → parcel → building. Borrowing ConfSeq's move, I would encode urban form in internal coordinates (relative bearings between street segments, façade-to-façade angles, setback ratios) rather than absolute lat/lon, giving a representation that is invariant to where on Earth the neighbourhood sits and therefore transferable between cities — the single hardest problem in cross-city urban-morphology modelling. A "UrbanSeq" token stream would let a plain transformer generate candidate neighbourhood morphologies conditioned on a density target, and feed the same tokens into accessibility prediction, replacing my current bespoke feature engineering.

② Make the discretization adaptive — and report its error budget honestly

Extending this paperEvery continuous angle in ConfSeq must be binned into a finite vocabulary, and that binning is the quiet hinge of the whole method: too coarse and reconstructed geometries drift out of physical validity, too fine and the vocabulary explodes while the model wastes capacity on chemically meaningless precision. The paper reports task performance but the accessible text gives no resolution-vs-accuracy curve. A strong follow-up: treat the tokenizer as learnable — a VQ / residual-VQ codebook over torsion space, or a variable-resolution scheme that spends bits where the torsional energy landscape is sharp (rotatable amide bonds) and saves them where it is flat (methyl rotors). Pair it with a published reconstruction error budget: RMSD attributable purely to quantization, separated from model error. That distinction is currently invisible and it determines how far the language can be trusted.

Added value to my researchThis is the geospatial MAUP problem wearing a new hat. My spatiotemporal kernel-density and grid-based work constantly faces the same question — H3 resolution, time-bin width, catchment radius — and the field still picks these by convention. Reframing spatial discretization as a learned tokenizer with a reported quantization-error budget gives me a principled, testable answer: learn a variable-resolution spatial codebook that spends resolution where the demand surface has sharp gradients (dense urban cores) and coarsens over homogeneous rural space. For 2SFCA specifically, this replaces the arbitrary fixed catchment with a data-driven partition, and it gives reviewers a number for how much of my prediction error is discretization rather than model.

③ Put physics back into the decoder — constrained generation instead of hopeful generation

Extending this paperConfSeq's own reference list flags the vulnerability: LLMs are demonstrably weak at arithmetic and symbolic induction (Qian et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024), and PoseBusters showed that AI-generated 3D poses routinely violate basic physics. A token model sampling torsions autoregressively has no mechanism preventing steric clashes, impossible ring closures, or energetically absurd conformers — validity is hoped for, not guaranteed. The extension: make decoding physics-constrained. Score partial token prefixes with a fast force field or learned energy model, mask tokens that would close a ring into a clash, and use constrained beam search or energy-guided sampling so the generator cannot emit an invalid structure in the first place. Then report validity and Boltzmann-weighted ensemble fidelity, not just RMSD-to-nearest — because a conformer set should match a distribution, not a point.

Added value to my researchDirect transplant into my physics-informed graph learning agenda. Spatial predictions have hard constraints that unconstrained models cheerfully violate: flow conservation in job–housing and commuting networks, non-negativity of demand, spatial contiguity of service areas, and the requirement that an allocated catchment be a connected region rather than a scatter of pixels. ConfSeq's "constrain at decode time" pattern is cleaner than my usual "penalize in the loss" approach — it makes violations impossible instead of merely expensive. Immediate target: constrained decoding for R2SFCA service-area delineation, so every generated catchment is contiguous and supply-balanced by construction.

④ Close the loop: from one-shot screening to an active-learning design–make–test cycle

Extending this paperThe STING and ALDH1B1 results are a single forward pass through the pipeline — generate, screen, buy, assay, report IC50. Nothing flows back. Yet the paper has assembled every component needed for a loop: a generative model over a readable token language, a shape-conditioned conditioning channel, and a validated assay readout. The extension is an active-learning cycle in which measured potencies fine-tune the ConfSeq generator (DPO/RL on token sequences, exactly as BindGPT does with reinforcement learning), the next batch is chosen by expected information gain under a limited synthesis budget, and the paper reports hit-rate improvement per round rather than a one-off hit list. That converts ConfSeq from a descriptor into a discovery engine, and it is the natural claim for the follow-up paper.

Added value to my researchMy spatial-planning work has precisely the same structure and the same missing loop. Siting EV chargers, clinics or telehealth hubs is generation-under-budget with an expensive, delayed, noisy real-world readout (utilization, appointment volume, uptake). Importing this design gives me a budgeted active-learning site-selection framework: propose candidate configurations, deploy a small batch, measure realized usage, update the generator. The scarce-validation-budget framing is the honest one for planning — a city cannot A/B test fifty sitings — and the "expected information gain per deployed unit" objective is a far better decision criterion than the static efficiency–equity Pareto front I currently optimize.

⑤ Cash in the readability: token-level attribution as native explainability, and text–geometry grounding

Extending this paperConfSeq's most under-exploited property is that its tokens are human-readable and chemically meaningful — a specific dihedral, a specific bond angle. That makes it a rare representation where attribution is interpretable by construction: attention and gradient attribution over ConfSeq tokens yield statements like "this torsion drives the predicted activity", which is a chemically actionable hypothesis, not a saliency blob over a point cloud. Two extensions follow. First, a conformational structure–activity map: systematically attribute QMugs property predictions and DUD-E/LIT-PCBA screening decisions back to individual torsion tokens, then verify the top-attributed torsions experimentally by rigidifying them. Second, because ConfSeq is text, it can share a vocabulary with natural language — enabling 3D-molecule ↔ text grounding ("generate a conformer with an extended lipophilic arm") that geometry-native models structurally cannot support.

Added value to my researchThis is the strongest argument I have seen for a token language for space, and it lands on two of my lines at once. (a) Explainable spatial ML: my MGWR/SHAP work explains in terms of variables, but a readable spatial token language would let attribution produce human-legible geographic statements — "the accessibility deficit is attributable to this street-connectivity token pattern" — which is genuinely closer to what a planner can act on than a coefficient surface. (b) Geospatial foundation models: sharing a tokenizer between space and text is the concrete mechanism for the LLM-plus-geography systems I want to fine-tune, letting a policy question in plain language be answered over an actual spatial structure rather than over a caption of one.
