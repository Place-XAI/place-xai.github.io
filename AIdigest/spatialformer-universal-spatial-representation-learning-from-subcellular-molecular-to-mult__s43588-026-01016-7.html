SpatialFormer: universal spatial representation learning from subcellular molecular to multicellular landscapes

Jun Wang · Yuanhua Huang · Ole Winther Published 2026-07-30 DOI 10.1038/s43588-026-01016-7 Source nature.com ↗
Core Conclusion

Existing single-cell foundation models treat expression as a bag of genes and largely discard where molecules and cells sit. SpatialFormer is a hybrid convolution + transformer encoder pretrained on 700 million cell pairs drawn from 17 million spatially resolved single cells across 71 Xenium slides, learning expression and subcellular gene spatial distribution jointly inside the cell's niche context via a pairwise training strategy. One pretrained representation then transfers across batch correction, cell-type annotation and cell–cell co-localization prediction, and — because co-localization is predicted rather than merely observed — the model supports in silico gene-pair perturbation that recovers immune cell–cell communication in pulmonary fibrosis, epithelial–myoepithelial co-localization and tumour-transition signals in breast cancer. The claim is that space is a pretraining signal, not a post-hoc annotation.

★

Why It Matters

  • Space enters the pretraining objective, not the analysis afterwards. Geneformer/scGPT-style models tokenize ranked expression; SpatialFormer additionally encodes subcellular transcript geometry and the surrounding niche, so spatial structure is learned rather than appended as a coordinate column.
  • The pairwise objective makes relations first-class. Training on cell pairs (700M of them) rather than isolated cells means the learned quantity is a relation — will these two cells co-localize? — which is exactly what downstream biology (communication, niche formation) cares about.
  • One representation, many tasks. Batch correction, cell-type annotation, niche annotation and co-localization detection are served by the same encoder, with reported zero-shot transfer to held-out Xenium slides and few-shot transfer to a different chemistry (MERFISH/MERSCOPE) and a different resolution regime (Visium HD).
  • Perturbation turns a descriptive atlas into a testable hypothesis generator. Ablating individual gene pairs and measuring the drop in predicted co-localization logit produces a ranked, FDR-controlled list of candidate interactions — recovering known ligand–receptor pairs and nominating novel ones (for example CXCL/CXCR3-axis and hyaluronan/CD44 biology in fibrotic granuloma).
  • Scale-bridging is the general lesson. The same architecture spans subcellular (transcript coordinates) → cellular (expression) → multicellular (niche). That is a template any spatial science can borrow, including urban and geographic analytics.

Core Methods

  • Hybrid CNN–transformer encoder. Convolutional blocks capture local molecular texture; transformer blocks capture long-range dependencies among genes and neighbouring cells. The design lineage is explicitly the Squeezeformer conv-transformer hybrid (Kim et al., NeurIPS 2022), adapted from speech to spatial omics.
  • Pairwise training strategy. The pretraining unit is a cell pair, not a cell: the model is asked whether two cells share a niche, so the learned embedding encodes relational/contextual structure directly. This is what makes co-localization a native prediction rather than a downstream clustering artefact.
  • Subcellular gene spatial features. Transcript-level coordinates are turned into gene–gene co-occurrence vs non-co-occurrence descriptors, so two cells with identical bulk expression but different intracellular transcript geometry receive different representations (Extended Data Fig. 1b).
  • Graph pretraining of the gene vocabulary. Global gene–gene interaction embeddings are generated with GraphSAGE over two-hop transcript neighbourhoods, supplying an informed initialization for gene tokens (Extended Data Fig. 1a).
  • Long-context and efficiency machinery. The reference list points to ALiBi position biases for length extrapolation, FlashAttention-2 for throughput, uncertainty-weighted multi-task loss (Kendall et al.) for balancing objectives, and LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning.
  • Differential co-occurrence testing (DGCO). Beyond differentially expressed genes, the paper defines differential gene-pair co-occurrence per cell type, ranked by co-occurrence strength with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control (Supplementary Table 2), and shows gene pairs can out-discriminate single genes for cell-type separation.
  • In silico perturbation of gene pairs. For each candidate pair, the change in predicted co-localization logit (the "deduction" d) is measured across all relevant cell pairs, tested with a one-tailed t-test and BH-adjusted; hits are cross-checked against a ligand–receptor database (NicheNet-style resources).
  • Transfer protocols. Zero-shot evaluation on unseen Xenium tissues and platforms; few-shot fine-tuning with 10k–100k same-slide cells for MERFISH; coordinate-perturbation sensitivity analysis (coordinate_perturbation.ipynb) probes robustness to positional noise.
  • Benchmarking. Against Nicheformer, CellContrast, PCA and four single-cell foundation models; metrics include accuracy, recall, precision, specificity, per-class F1 and Jensen–Shannon divergence between predicted and reference cell-type co-localization distributions; integration assessed with scIB-style benchmarks and BBKNN comparisons.
  • Ablation on pretraining scale. Three checkpoints of increasing corpus breadth — a single lung slide, all lung slides, mixed-tissue slides — are compared, isolating how much of the performance is bought by data diversity rather than architecture (Fig. 3d,e).

① Make the niche radius a learned, anisotropic field instead of a fixed 30 µm patch

Extending this paperSpatialFormer pretrains on 30 µm patches and then evaluates across radii of 10–120 µm — an admission that the true interaction range is tissue-, cell-type- and mechanism-dependent (diffusion-limited paracrine signalling reaches farther than juxtacrine contact). The natural extension is to stop treating radius as a hyperparameter and let the model emit a per-cell-pair interaction kernel: predict, jointly with the co-localization logit, a distance-decay profile whose bandwidth is conditioned on cell-type identity and local density. Anisotropy matters too — alveolar septa, ductal architecture and fibrotic scars impose directional structure that an isotropic disc erases. Concretely: replace the fixed patch with a learned kernel head, supervise it with the multi-radius sweep the paper already computed (Fig. 3d and Extended Data Fig. 3 source data are exactly the training signal needed), and report whether the learned bandwidth recovers known signalling ranges. The pretraining-scale ablation (single slide → all lung → mixed tissue) would then have a second axis: does corpus diversity buy range generalization, not just accuracy?

Added value to my researchThis is my 2SFCA/R2SFCA catchment problem in a different substrate, and the isomorphism is close enough to be operational. A 30 µm niche is a fixed catchment radius; the 10–120 µm sweep is exactly the sensitivity analysis I run over 15/30/60-minute service areas; the "true radius varies by cell type" finding is the same objection that kills one-size-fits-all catchments for primary care versus specialist mental-health services. Porting the learned-kernel head gives me a demand-and-supply-conditioned distance decay — bandwidth predicted from local population density, transit availability and provider type rather than assumed Gaussian — which is precisely the piece of R2SFCA that has always been hand-tuned. The anisotropy point transfers verbatim: rivers, highways and administrative boundaries deform service areas the way tissue architecture deforms niches, and an anisotropic kernel is a cleaner fix than network-distance-only routing. It also connects to my MGWR work: a learned bandwidth field is a spatial-non-stationarity estimate, and comparing it against MGWR's cross-validated bandwidths would be a genuinely novel methodological bridge between GeoAI and classic spatial statistics.

② Turn in silico gene-pair perturbation into a causal-identification protocol

Extending this paperThe paper's most striking move is ablating a gene pair and reading off the drop in predicted co-localization logit, then FDR-testing the deduction across macrophage–T-cell pairs. But this measures the model's sensitivity, not the tissue's causality: a pair can carry high logit weight because it is a marker of the cell types that happen to co-localize, which is confounding, not communication. The paper partially guards against this by flagging ligand–receptor overlap, yet the volcano plots still show many significant "other" pairs whose status is ambiguous. Three extensions would harden it. (a) Counterfactual balancing: perturb the pair while holding cell-type identity fixed, so the deduction isolates niche signal from identity signal. (b) Double perturbation to detect interaction/epistasis between pairs — does knocking out CXCL9–CXCR3 change the effect of hyaluronan–CD44, indicating a shared pathway? (c) External validation against actual perturbation data: an optimal-transport perturbation model can predict the expression state after a real knockdown, and the two predictions can be required to agree. That converts a saliency map into a falsifiable causal claim.

Added value to my researchThis is a directly importable template for my explainable ML for spatial prediction line, where I have the identical confound: SHAP or permutation importance on a spatial model tells me the model leans on green-space share or provider density, not that changing them would change health outcomes. The paper's three-part discipline — perturb, FDR-control across many units, then check against an external knowledge base of known mechanisms — is a protocol I can adopt wholesale for telehealth-uptake and mental-health-accessibility models: perturb broadband availability or clinic supply in a fitted model, test the deduction across all census tracts with BH correction, and validate hits against known policy interventions rather than accepting a ranked feature list. The double-perturbation idea is the more interesting import: spatial policy levers interact strongly (adding a clinic where no transit reaches it does nothing), and a pairwise-perturbation grid over levers would surface those complementarities in a way single-feature importance never can.

③ Stress-test "universal" against the platform and resolution gap it half-crosses

Extending this paperThe title says universal, but the evidence is asymmetric: pretraining is entirely Xenium (71 slides), zero-shot transfer works on held-out Xenium tissues, and MERFISH/MERSCOPE required few-shot fine-tuning with 10k–100k cells to reach comparable performance. That gap is the paper's most informative negative result and deserves promotion from a caveat to an experiment. What exactly does the model fail to transfer — the gene vocabulary (different panels, different plex), the transcript-detection sensitivity, or the spatial statistics of segmentation? A clean extension: hold out an entire platform rather than a tissue, sweep the few-shot budget below 10k to find the adaptation floor, and decompose the domain shift by separately re-fitting the gene embedding, the conv stem and the niche head to see which module absorbs the shift. Adding Visium HD and imaging-based modalities as pretraining rather than evaluation domains would test whether a genuinely multi-platform corpus removes the need for fine-tuning at all — the single most valuable follow-up for anyone who wants to use these weights off the shelf.

Added value to my researchPlatform shift here is sensor and jurisdiction shift in my work, and I hit it constantly: a model trained on US census + Sentinel-2 + Twitter-derived mobility does not survive transplant to a Chinese city with Baidu-heat data, different administrative units and a different POI ontology. The paper's decomposition strategy — figure out which module owns the domain gap — is exactly the diagnostic I lack when a fine-tuned geospatial foundation model degrades across regions. Its finding that the gene vocabulary is the likely bottleneck maps to my POI-taxonomy problem: harmonizing category schemas across data providers is the analogue of harmonizing gene panels, and both argue for a learned, alignable vocabulary embedding rather than a fixed one-hot ontology. The few-shot budget curve is also a concrete deliverable for my open-science/FAIR line: reporting "how many local labels does a transferred model need" is far more useful to a public-health agency than a single headline accuracy, and I can adopt that reporting convention directly.

④ Add time and a mechanistic prior: from static niches to a physics-informed spatiotemporal operator

Extending this paperEvery slide SpatialFormer sees is a fixed section — a single frame of a process that is intrinsically dynamic. Fibrotic granulomas form, immune cells migrate, tumour cells transit through the "transitional" state the paper itself names in the breast-cancer analysis. The learned co-localization is therefore a snapshot of an equilibrium whose kinetics are invisible. Two coupled extensions: (a) serial-section or time-course pretraining, so the pairwise objective becomes "will these cells co-localize next", turning the model into a niche-dynamics predictor; and (b) a physics-informed constraint that encodes what is already known about the mechanism — the effective communication range is set by the ratio of cytokine secretion to diffusion time constants (a result cited in the paper's own reference list), which is a soft constraint the network could be forced to respect rather than rediscover from data. A diffusion-consistency penalty on the learned interaction kernel would both regularize the small-sample regime and make extrapolation to unseen tissues principled instead of empirical.

Added value to my researchThis is the centre of my physics-informed graph learning and spatiotemporal KDE agenda, and the biological framing sharpens it. The secretion-versus-diffusion constant that sets interaction range is formally the same object as the decay parameter in my spatiotemporal kernel density estimates of disease or service demand, and the paper's willingness to bind a learned kernel to a known transport law is exactly what I have argued for against purely data-driven GNNs. The "predict the next configuration" reformulation transfers to job-housing network evolution and to agent-based models of care-seeking: pairwise co-location prediction over time is the microscopic version of an ABM transition rule, which suggests a hybrid where a learned pairwise operator supplies the interaction rules an ABM normally hard-codes. Practically, an SIR-style or diffusion-style conservation penalty added to a spatial GNN is a small implementation change with a large payoff in extrapolation — the same argument the paper would need to make to defend "universal" beyond its Xenium corpus.

⑤ Reuse the pairwise-niche objective as a general spatial pretext task for urban and health geography

Extending this paperStrip SpatialFormer of its biology and what remains is a broadly reusable recipe: tokenize a spatial unit by its attribute vector plus its subunit geometry, pretrain on whether two units share a neighbourhood, and read off relations rather than labels. The paper never tests that abstraction, and it should — a decisive demonstration would be to apply the identical architecture to a non-omics spatial point pattern and show the pairwise pretext task still yields transferable embeddings. Internally, there is a cheaper version of the same test: the paper shows gene pairs (DGCO) out-discriminate single genes for cell typing, which is a claim about co-occurrence being a richer descriptor than marginal intensity. Formalizing that — deriving when pairwise co-occurrence statistics dominate marginals, as a function of sparsity and spatial autocorrelation — would give the architecture a theoretical rationale rather than an empirical one, and would tell practitioners in any spatial field when the extra cost of pairwise training is justified.

Added value to my researchThis is the idea I would actually build. Replace cells with POIs, buildings or census blocks, replace subcellular transcript coordinates with intra-parcel land-use composition or building footprint geometry, replace the 30 µm niche with a walkable buffer or street-network neighbourhood, and the pairwise objective becomes "do these two POIs belong to the same functional neighbourhood" — a self-supervised pretext task that needs no labels, which is the binding constraint in urban analytics. That yields pretrained neighbourhood embeddings I can fine-tune for 15-minute-city delineation, job-housing balance, healthy-city typologies or EV-charging siting, all from the same encoder. The DGCO result maps to POI co-location patterns: pairs of amenities (pharmacy + clinic, gym + grocery) plausibly characterize neighbourhood function better than amenity counts alone, which is a testable hypothesis I can run on open OSM data tomorrow and which reframes spatial entropy and co-location mining as a pretraining objective rather than a descriptive statistic. The perturbation machinery then comes free: ablate an amenity pair and measure the drop in predicted neighbourhood coherence — an explainable, equity-auditable siting tool.
