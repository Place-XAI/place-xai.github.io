Zero-shot design of drug-binding proteins via neural iterative selection−expansion

Benjamin Fry · Kaia Slaw · Nicholas F. Polizzi Published 2026-06-24 DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10670-w Source nature.com ↗
Core Conclusion

Closing the loop between two reciprocal neural networks — a ligand-aware sequence designer (LASErMPNN) and a protein–ligand co-structure predictor (RFAA or Boltz-2) — makes zero-shot design of small-molecule binding proteins work. The NISE algorithm (neural iterative selection–expansion) climbs the joint distribution P(sequence, structure, ligand conformation) instead of a physics energy function, and produced binders to two chemically unrelated drugs with 100% (exatecan, 4/4) and 83% (apixaban, 5/6) experimental success rates. Affinities reached 1.2 nM (exatecan, after purely computational "neural proofreading" of two residues) and 80 pM (apixaban) — roughly 70× and ~10,000× tighter than the previous best methods — and the designed binder kept exatecan's labile lactone ring intact for >50 h.

★

Why It Matters

  • Small-molecule binder design stops being a numbers game. The competing NTF2/LigandMPNN+Rosetta pipeline tested 9,024 designs to get 4 binders; NISE tested 6 and got 5, on the same published backbones and the same target. Hit rate improved by more than three orders of magnitude.
  • Neural networks beat the energy function, and the paper shows why. Swapping the co-structure predictor for Rosetta energy minimisation (energy-based ISE) failed to improve either ligand confidence or sequence likelihood — evidence that empirical energy gradients point partly orthogonal to the learned joint distribution.
  • Affinity maturation without any experiment. LASErMPNN "proofreading" nominated Q51N and M97L purely from likelihood; each gave >10× affinity, and combined they were energetically additive (100×, Kd = 1.2 nM). A control mutation predicted to be bad (G104M) indeed bound 57 µM.
  • Function, not just binding. EPIC(Q51N/M97L) sequesters exatecan's hydrolysis-prone lactone, holding >99% of the drug in its bioactive ring-closed form for ≥50 h, whereas free drug hydrolyses with a ~2 h plasma half-life and HSA binding does not protect it. This is a delivery/sponge reagent, not a benchmark number.
  • Verified by crystallography. Two X-ray structures (2.0 Å and 2.2 Å) confirm the designed binding mode, with binding-site Cα r.m.s.d. 0.4 Å and ligand displacement <1 Å from the design model — the algorithm's intent was physically realised.
  • Cheap, reproducible compute. A 14-iteration NISE trajectory takes about 5 h on four A6000 GPUs, with code, Colab notebooks and processed training data released openly.

  • A long-standing barrier falls: zero-shot de novo design of small-molecule binders had resisted deep learning; this is a qualitative capability change, not an incremental benchmark gain.
  • Head-to-head, apples-to-apples comparison: the apixaban experiment reuses the previously published NTF2 backbones and target, so the improvement cannot be attributed to a better starting point — exactly the falsifiability Nature rewards.
  • Algorithm + mechanism + experiment in one arc: a probabilistic argument (coordinate ascent on a joint distribution), a trained architecture, wet-lab affinities, crystal structures, and a functional assay (hydrolysis protection).

  • Open science: models, training code, dataset splits, design coordinates and notebooks are all public — reusable infrastructure for the field.

Held-out benchmarks
The streptavidin fold was rigorously excluded by sequence, structure and evolutionary similarity; the de novo rucaparib binder PiB (PDB 8TN6) was held out from both LASErMPNN and RFAA training. Key results: up to 94% binding-site sequence recovery for streptavidin (top-25 of 10,000 designs); 74% binding-site / 80% core recovery for PiB, with the native PiB sequence ranked 4th of 1,001.

NISE sampling: 1,000 LASErMPNN sequences per selected structure per round, top 3 structures carried forward; the benchmark trajectory in Fig. 1c used n = 1,500 designs per iteration (500 in round 1) over 35 rounds; Fig. 3e density plots use n = 4,000. Apixaban: 6 NISE designs tested, 5 bound with Kd < 50 nM; APEX Kd = 80 pM (95% CI 54–122 pM), 13 kDa versus factor Xa's 43 kDa.

Core Methods

  • NISE — neural iterative selection–expansion: alternate expansion (sample many sequences from P(sequence | structure, ligand) at high softmax temperature) with selection (predict co-structures, keep only self-consistent designs, then take a confidence-based argmax over P(structure, ligand | sequence)). Formally an iterated conditional-modes / coordinate-ascent climb on the joint distribution P(sequence, structure, ligand conformation).
  • LASErMPNN: a heterograph encoder–decoder message-passing network with separate protein and ligand node types. It autoregressively decodes amino-acid identity and side-chain dihedrals (χ1–χ4) jointly, in random decoding order, conditioning each residue on previously decoded identities and rotamers.
  • Pretrained, frozen ligand encoder: trained to predict quantum-chemistry-derived atomic properties (e.g. partial charge) from coordinates and elements, then locked — giving the designer chemical awareness of ligands it has never seen, plus a diagnostic read-out that LigandMPNN lacks.
  • Frame-level backbone noising: backbones are idealised and then whole residue "frames" are noised (rather than atoms independently), which suppresses memorisation of crystallographic artefacts and enables accurate dihedral decoding under backbone noise.
  • Self-consistency filtering: keep designs whose predicted co-structure matches the input in both backbone Cα and ligand heavy-atom r.m.s.d. (< 2.0–2.5 Å). The paper shows ligand-level self-consistency is a far more stringent screen than backbone-only self-consistency.
  • Confidence-based ranking: ligand pLDDT as the primary objective, plus Boltz-2's binary affinity probability P(bind) in a composite score for apixaban; final designs are re-ranked by a biophysical penalty on buried, non-hydrogen-bonded polar atoms.
  • Neural proofreading: revisit each binding-site residue at low temperature with all other residues fixed, on an Amber-relaxed RFAA model, and accept single substitutions that lower the negative log-likelihood — in-silico affinity maturation with no experimental feedback.
  • Scaffold generation: parametric four-helix coiled coils + RFdiffusion loops + ProteinMPNN sequences + AF2 validation; ligand placement by COMBS/van der Mers (exatecan) or brute-force rigid-body docking, CARPdock (apixaban).
  • Ablation and baseline controls: ligand-free LASErMPNN, retrained LigandMPNN, and energy-based ISE (Rosetta minimisation in place of the co-structure predictor) — the last isolating the contribution of the second neural network.
  • Wet-lab validation: E. coli expression, SEC monomericity, fluorescence polarisation/anisotropy Kd fitting with bootstrapped confidence intervals, competition assays, X-ray crystallography, and time-resolved absorbance for lactone hydrolysis kinetics.

① Turn NISE from a hand-tuned loop into a learned selection–expansion controller

Extending this paperNISE's schedule is fixed by hand: 1,000 sequences per structure, top-3 by ligand pLDDT, a preset softmax temperature, 14–35 iterations. The paper itself notes that both EPIC and APEX emerged from late iterations, i.e. after the pLDDT plateau — so the stopping rule and the exploration/exploitation balance are currently guesswork. A direct extension: treat the loop as a bandit / RL controller whose actions are (temperature, branching factor, which parents to keep, when to stop) and whose reward is downstream biophysical quality rather than raw confidence, and learn it once across many targets so compute is spent where the joint distribution is actually still improving. Because NISE is explicitly network-agnostic, the same controller would carry over to RFdiffusion-AA/BoltzGen backbone generators as inputs.

Added value to my researchThis is exactly the structure of my facility-siting and EV-charging optimisation work: a generator proposes configurations, a predictor scores them, and today both the search schedule and the stopping rule are ad hoc. Recasting R2SFCA catchment design and charging-station siting as a selection–expansion loop with a learned controller — expand many candidate spatial configurations, select by a predictor's calibrated confidence, iterate — gives me a principled replacement for greedy heuristics, and a compute budget I can defend in a paper.

② Audit and calibrate the confidence surrogate that NISE optimises

Extending this paperThe whole method rests on ligand pLDDT as a proxy for binding. The paper is admirably candid that this proxy is imperfect: across the five verified apixaban binders, ligand pLDDT correlated only weakly with measured affinity, and Boltz-2's P(bind) separated on- from off-target ligands but did not rank affinities; RFAA-based scoring would have caused them to discard the apixaban binders entirely. The obvious extension is a calibration study: pool the growing set of designs with measured Kd, fit reliability curves for pLDDT / iPAE / P(bind) / pAffinity, and replace the linear composite score with a probabilistically calibrated, uncertainty-aware objective — turning "climb the confidence" into "climb a score whose error bars are known".

Added value to my researchIdentical pathology in my spatial prediction work: I optimise and select on model confidence (or R²) that is not the quantity policymakers care about. This motivates a calibration-first explainable-ML protocol for spatial accessibility models — reliability diagrams for predicted access scores against observed utilisation, and selection rules that carry uncertainty through to the equity conclusion rather than reporting a point estimate.

③ Generalise "neural proofreading" into a minimal-intervention explainability engine

Extending this paperProofreading is the paper's most under-exploited result: fixing every residue but one and re-scoring at low temperature nominated two substitutions worth 100× in affinity, and correctly predicted a deleterious one (G104M, 57 µM). That is a full single-site counterfactual scan being used only to pick two mutations. Extended properly it becomes a saliency map over the binding site — a per-residue ΔNLL landscape, epistasis probed by pairwise double scans (the paper only shows additivity for one pair), and an explicit test of whether ΔNLL predicts ΔΔG across many designs. That would convert an anecdote into a validated in-silico affinity-maturation operator, and expose which chemical interactions the network has actually learned.

Added value to my researchThis is a clean template for my explainable-GeoAI agenda: instead of post-hoc SHAP on a black box, do minimal-intervention counterfactual scans on the spatial configuration itself — remove or relocate one clinic, one charging station, one transit link, and read the model's likelihood change. The ΔNLL-vs-ΔΔG validation logic maps directly onto validating counterfactual spatial attributions against observed changes in utilisation after a real facility opened or closed.

④ Add explicit negative design: optimise the specificity gap, not just on-target confidence

Extending this paperThe authors show that pure positive design already yields useful specificity (EPIC rejects apixaban and dexamethasone; APEX rejects exatecan) and they sketch — but never run — the obvious upgrade: select each round on the difference in ligand pLDDT / predicted affinity between the target and a panel of off-targets. The hard case is right there in their own data: EPIC still binds FL118, belotecan and camptothecin at 6–90 µM, and the structure–activity series quantifies exactly which substituents drive the gap. Running NISE with a multi-ligand objective over the camptothecin family — or over apixaban vs. rivaroxaban/edoxaban — would test whether the joint-distribution climb can be steered to discriminate near-isosteres, which is the requirement for any real biosensor or antidote.

Added value to my researchMulti-objective selection with a competing panel is the accessibility problem in disguise: a facility configuration should serve a target population and not simply reallocate access away from a comparison group. Formalising my efficiency-vs-equity trade-off as an explicit positive/negative design gap — maximise access for the underserved cohort while bounding loss for others, iterated over rounds — is a cleaner objective than the weighted sums I currently use, and yields an interpretable "specificity" metric for spatial interventions.

⑤ Follow the designed antidote out of the lab: from zero-shot binder to access-equity question

Extending this paperThe apixaban work is explicitly motivated by an unmet clinical need — a cheap reversal agent after andexanet alfa's withdrawal — and APEX is a 13 kDa E. coli-expressed protein binding at 80 pM, roughly rivalling factor Xa itself. The paper stops at in vitro affinity. The natural extension chain is (i) in vivo reversal pharmacology and immunogenicity, (ii) a generalisation stress test: run the identical zero-shot pipeline across a panel of drugs with no structural precedent to measure how success rate degrades with chemical novelty and scaffold-pocket mismatch, and (iii) release the resulting design/affinity pairs as a FAIR benchmark so the field can measure zero-shot generalisation rather than report single successes.
