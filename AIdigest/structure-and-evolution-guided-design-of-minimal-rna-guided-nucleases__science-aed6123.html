Structure and evolution-guided design of minimal RNA-guided nucleases

Petr Skopintsev · Isabel Esain-Garcia · Evan C. DeTurk · Peter H. Yoon · Zehan Zhou · Trevor Weiss · Maris Kamalu · Ajit Chamraj · Kenneth J. Loi · Conner J. Langeberg · Ron S. Core Conclusion

Coupling a structure-guided inverse-folding model with evolution-informed residue constraints produces SynTnpBs — artificial, sequence-divergent variants of the minimal CRISPR-Cas12-like nuclease TnpB that remain enzymatically active. High-throughput screening of the AI-generated library yielded editors that retained or exceeded wild-type activity in bacterial, plant, and human cells, and cryo-EM of the most divergent variant revealed stabilizing contacts at the RNA–DNA interfaces across conformational states. The result: multidomain, conformationally dynamic nucleic-acid binders are now designable, enlarging the accessible protein space beyond what evolution has sampled.

★

Why It Matters

  • Breaks the "one static domain" ceiling of protein design. Most successful de novo design targets small, rigid, single-domain folds. This work designs a multidomain enzyme that must change conformation while holding a guide RNA and a DNA target — a far harder objective, and it works.
  • Design constraints, not just design models. The novelty is the coupling: an inverse-folding model proposes sequences, and evolution-derived residue constraints keep the proposals inside functionally viable regions. Neither ingredient alone reliably yields active multidomain nucleases.
  • Editors that beat the natural template. Retaining or exceeding wild-type activity in three very different cellular chassis (bacteria, plants, human cells) is the hard evidence that the designs are functional tools, not merely folded proteins.
  • Compactness is the deployment argument. TnpB is a minimal Cas12-like nuclease; expanding the designable space around it directly serves delivery-constrained applications (AAV-packaged therapy, transgene-free plant editing) where Cas9's size is the bottleneck.
  • Mechanistic closure via cryo-EM. Solving the structure of the most divergent variant turns a black-box success into an interpretable one — the paper names which interfacial contacts stabilize the designed enzyme, giving the next design round a physical target.
  • Escapes the natural-diversity ceiling. Prior TnpB engineering mined natural homologs; this establishes that useful variants can be generated rather than found, decoupling tool discovery from the pace of metagenomic sampling.

Core Methods

  • Structure-guided inverse folding. A fixed-backbone sequence-design model (the ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN/ESM-IF family cited by the paper) proposes amino-acid sequences that should fold into the TnpB backbone, rather than mutating the wild-type sequence residue by residue.
  • Evolution-informed residue constraints. Positions and pairs identified as functionally coupled across the natural TnpB family (coevolution / direct-coupling statistics) are held or restricted, so the generator is free to diverge in the "tolerant" sequence space while conserving the catalytic and nucleic-acid-binding machinery. This coupling is the paper's methodological core.
  • Controlled divergence. Designs are pushed toward large sequence distance from wild type — the abstract's framing ("divergent variants", "most divergent variant") implies divergence was an explicit design axis, not a byproduct.
  • High-throughput functional screening. The AI-generated library is screened for nuclease/editing activity, converting a generative-model output distribution into an empirically ranked hit list; amplicon sequencing (CRISPResso2-style analysis) quantifies editing.
  • Cross-chassis validation. Hits are re-tested in bacterial, plant, and human cells — three delivery and chromatin contexts — establishing that activity is a property of the protein, not of one permissive assay.
  • Cryo-EM structure determination of the extreme case. Single-particle cryo-EM on the most divergent active variant, resolved in multiple conformational states, identifies stabilizing contacts at the RNA–DNA interfaces — closing the loop from generative proposal back to physical mechanism.
  • Method positioning. Constraint-regularized generative design + massively parallel experimental selection + structural interpretation: a design–build–test triad in which the "test" is high-throughput and the "explain" step is atomic-resolution.

① Make the evolutionary constraint a tunable dial and map the divergence–activity frontier

Extending this paperThe paper's decisive move is coupling an inverse-folding generator to evolution-informed residue constraints — but the coupling is reported as a working recipe, not as a characterized operating curve. The obvious extension is to treat constraint strength as a continuous knob (fraction of coupled positions frozen, coupling-score threshold, temperature of the design model) and trace the resulting Pareto frontier of sequence divergence versus retained activity, per domain: RuvC catalytic core, ωRNA-binding module, TAM-recognition surface. That converts "SynTnpBs work" into a transferable design law — how much evolutionary prior is the minimum needed for a multidomain nucleic-acid enzyme to survive generation? — and would immediately tell the field whether the same dial setting generalizes to Cas12f, IscB, or Fanzor.

Added value to my researchThis is exactly the structure of my constrained spatial-generation problems. An MSA prior over residue couplings is formally the same object as an empirical covariance/adjacency prior over urban units: both regularize a generator toward configurations that the observed world has demonstrated are viable. Porting the dial gives me a principled way to generate counterfactual service layouts (clinics, EV chargers, green space) that diverge boldly from the status quo yet stay inside a "spatially viable" manifold learned from real cities — with an explicit knob trading novelty against realism, which is precisely what planning stakeholders ask for and what unconstrained generative urban models fail to provide.

② Close the loop: replace one-shot library design with active, uncertainty-driven rounds

Extending this paperThe reported workflow is essentially open-loop — generate a library, screen it, pick winners. The screen's negative results (inactive designs) are as informative as the hits, yet nothing in the described pipeline feeds them back into the generator. Extending the paper: run iterative design–build–test–learn rounds in which each screen trains a surrogate activity model over the SynTnpB sequence space, and the next batch is chosen by an acquisition function balancing predicted activity, divergence, and model uncertainty. Two quantities become the headline metrics: hit rate per experimental batch, and the maximum achievable divergence at fixed experimental budget. Given how expensive multi-chassis editing assays are, batch efficiency — not model size — is the real bottleneck.

Added value to my researchThe acquisition mathematics is identical to my siting problems: choose the next k locations to place a charger, a clinic, or an air-quality sensor when each placement is costly and the response surface is non-stationary. Framing my EV-charging efficiency–equity optimization and 2SFCA-informed facility siting as batch Bayesian experimental design over space (rather than one-shot optimization on a frozen demand surface) gives a defensible answer to "where do we build the next ten?", and lets explainable-ML attribution run on the surrogate to say which built-environment features are actually buying the accessibility gain.

③ Design against the conformational ensemble, not a single backbone

Extending this paperThe most striking result is structural: the divergent variant's stabilizing contacts appear across conformations. But the design step used structure-guided inverse folding, which conventionally conditions on one backbone. The paper therefore succeeded at a multi-state objective using a single-state generator — meaning the evolutionary constraints were implicitly carrying the multi-state information. Extending it: condition the generator explicitly on an ensemble of TnpB states (apo, RNA-bound, DNA-engaged, cleavage-competent), score each candidate by worst-case rather than mean-state compatibility, and test whether ensemble-conditioned designs achieve higher activity at equal divergence. This is the difference between designing a shape and designing a machine.

Added value to my researchSingle-state versus ensemble design is the exact failure mode of accessibility analysis. Almost all 2SFCA/R2SFCA studies — mine included — evaluate one "conformation" of the urban system: a static, typically weekday-daytime supply-and-demand snapshot. The right analogue is to compute accessibility over an ensemble of spatiotemporal states (weekday/weekend, day/night, term/holiday, disruption and surge states) and optimize the worst-case or state-averaged surface. That reframes my spatiotemporal KDE and resilience work as multi-state design, and it produces a policy metric planners lack: how much accessibility a neighborhood retains in its worst state, not its average one.

④ Pre-rank designs with a fitness model, and ask honestly whether scale helps out of distribution

Extending this paperTnpB is a minimal, transposon-associated family: shallow, patchy, and poorly represented relative to the giant families protein language models are usually benchmarked on — and SynTnpBs are deliberately pushed off the natural distribution. This makes the paper an unusually sharp test bed for a question the field keeps dodging: do larger sequence models actually improve variant-effect prediction when the family is data-poor and the candidates are non-natural? The extension is to score the entire generated library with fitness predictors of increasing scale before the wet-lab screen, then use the screen as ground truth to measure where the scaling curve flattens or inverts. The paper's own screening data would turn into one of the few genuinely out-of-distribution benchmarks in the field.

Added value to my researchThis is my geospatial-foundation-model question in another costume. I fine-tune large pretrained models for spatial prediction targets that are data-sparse, spatially non-stationary, and often extrapolative (a new city, a new policy regime) — and I need an evidence-based answer to when scale pays versus when a small, domain-constrained model with explicit spatial priors wins. Adopting this paper's protocol — hold out an off-distribution evaluation set by construction, then plot performance against model scale — gives me a transferable evaluation design for geospatial foundation-model fine-tuning, and a principled defense of hybrid physics-informed models where scaling stalls.

⑤ From designable editors to reachable therapies: pair compactness with an access geography

Extending this paperThe paper's translational logic rests on minimality — a compact nuclease is easier to package and deliver. Extending it: make delivery an explicit design objective rather than an inherited property, co-optimizing SynTnpB size, immunogenic-epitope load, and vector compatibility (AAV cargo limits, LNP-mRNA length, plant viral vectors for transgene-free germline editing), and reporting activity per delivered dose across chassis instead of per transfection. A second, non-negotiable extension for AI-generated nucleases: provenance and biosafety — a registry linking each designed sequence to its generator, constraints, and off-target profile, so that "non-natural" editors entering the literature remain auditable.

Added value to my researchMolecular designability is only half of health benefit; the other half is geographic. Gene-therapy and advanced-editing capacity is concentrated in a handful of academic medical centers, so a technology that halves manufacturing or delivery cost has a spatially uneven benefit incidence. This gives me a concrete new application for my health-geography stack: a 2SFCA/R2SFCA accessibility analysis of gene-therapy-capable facilities, coupled to travel-burden and telehealth-substitution modeling, that estimates who actually gains when editors get smaller and cheaper. It connects my digital-divide and specialty-care access work to the frontier of therapeutic innovation — precisely the space-human coupling question my program exists to answer.
