Large language models exhibit stigmatizing behaviour in contextual judgements of health conditions

Xi Wang · Yujia Zhou · Guangyu Zhou Published 2026-07-06 DOI 10.1038/s44360-026-00164-4 Source nature.com ↗
Core Conclusion

LLMs look cleaner than humans on explicit stigma scales — across six models and six scales they scored below the meta-analytic human benchmark (Nhuman = 56,612) — yet the same models show systematic stigma-congruent behaviour when asked to make situated decisions. In a contextual judgement task of 61,200 model decisions across 51 scenarios, differences versus healthy baselines were largest for mental-health disorders and HIV/HBV. Reasoning-enabled models showed smaller condition gaps, and prompting strategies distilled from their reasoning traces transferred to non-reasoning models across languages and scenarios. The headline is a dissociation between what a model says and how it decides — so declarative fairness benchmarks overestimate deployment safety, and audits must be context-sensitive.

★

Why It Matters

  • It invalidates the dominant safety evidence. Most health-AI fairness reporting rests on explicit questionnaires ("do you agree people with HIV should…"). This paper shows a model can pass every one of those and still discriminate the moment it is asked to choose — hire, house, seat, trust, refer. Passing a stigma scale is now a necessary, not sufficient, condition.
  • It measures behaviour, not stated attitude. Moving from six scales to 61,200 situated decisions across 51 scenarios converts a values question into a measurable behavioural quantity, with a human benchmark attached (56,612 participants) so model and human can be placed on the same axis.
  • It targets the conditions where stigma actually kills. HIV, hepatitis B and mental-health disorders are precisely the domains where anticipated stigma suppresses testing, disclosure and treatment-seeking. A biased triage or advice layer here does not just offend — it removes people from care pathways.
  • It offers a mitigation that costs nothing. Rather than only diagnosing, the authors mine reasoning-model traces for prompting strategies and show these lower stigma-congruent output in cheaper non-reasoning models, across languages. That is a deployable patch for settings that cannot afford frontier reasoning inference.
  • It generalizes beyond health. The explicit-versus-contextual dissociation is a template auditors can port to any domain where an LLM allocates a scarce good — clinic referral, benefits eligibility, housing, credit — including spatially targeted service allocation.

Core Methods

  • Dual-track audit design (the paper's core move): run the same models through (i) explicit stigma scales and (ii) contextual judgement vignettes, then measure the dissociation between the two tracks. The contrast — not either track alone — is the evidence.
  • Human-anchored psychometrics: administer six validated stigma instruments to LLMs as if they were respondents, and score them against a meta-analytic human benchmark (N = 56,612) rather than against other models (Fig. 2).
  • Factorial scenario manipulation: across 51 scenarios, hold the situation fixed and vary the target's health condition (HIV / HBV / mental-health disorder / healthy baseline), so any shift in the decision is attributable to condition alone — a vignette-experiment logic imported from social psychology into model auditing.
  • Stigma-congruent response rate as the outcome: code each of the 61,200 decisions as congruent or not with a stigmatizing stance, giving a single rate comparable across models, languages, scenarios and conditions (Fig. 3).
  • Reasoning-mode contrast: compare reasoning-enabled versus standard generation on the identical task to test whether deliberation narrows condition gaps (Fig. 4) — an ablation on the inference process, not the training data.
  • Reasoning-trace mining → transferable prompts: read the reasoning content of the models that do behave better, extract the strategies they use, compile them into prompts, and test whether those prompts lower stigma-congruent output in non-reasoning models, across languages and scenarios. This is distillation of a behavioural safeguard rather than of capability.
  • Cross-lingual replication: repeat the contextual task in more than one language to separate a genuine behavioural regularity from an artefact of English-language training corpora.

① Give the 51 scenarios a place: geo-contextual stigma auditing

Extending this paperThe paper's scenarios are placeless — a person with HBV seeking a job, a flatmate, a seat — with health condition as the only manipulated factor. But health-related stigma is famously geographic: HBV discrimination against rural migrant workers in Beijing and HIV stigma in rural Uganda (both in the paper's own reference list) are place-specific phenomena, not universal ones. The direct extension is a third manipulated factor: setting — rural clinic vs. tertiary urban hospital, migrant-receiving city vs. sending county, high-resource vs. low-resource health system — turning the 61,200-decision design into a scenario × condition × place factorial. If stigma-congruent rates move with the described setting, then a deployed health LLM is not one biased system but a spatially heterogeneous field of biases, and the paper's "context-sensitive audit" call acquires an explicit geographic axis.

Added value to my researchThis is a new instrument for my health-geography line. Today my accessibility work treats a clinic as a supply point with a capacity number; if an LLM triage or advice layer sits in front of that clinic and its stigma-congruent rate varies by described setting, then effective supply is condition- and place-dependent. I can build an "algorithmic access penalty" term and fold it into R2SFCA: supplyj is discounted by the model's measured willingness to refer someone with HIV/mental illness in a setting resembling j. That converts an AI-fairness finding into a mappable accessibility surface — exactly the coupling of space-human systems my program targets.

② From static vignettes to adversarial, self-refreshing stigma benchmarks

Extending this paperA fixed bank of 51 scenarios has a shelf life: once published (and the data are openly on OSF), it becomes training-set-adjacent, and the next model generation can pass it without becoming less stigmatizing — Goodhart's law applied to fairness audits. The extension is to make the paper's benchmark generative and adversarial: keep the scenario schema (situation type × condition × language × setting) but synthesize fresh instances continuously, with an attacker model searching the scenario space for the region where a target model's stigma-congruent rate peaks. The paper's own finding — that reasoning traces contain reusable mitigation strategies — implies the reverse is also minable: traces should contain the features that trigger stigma-congruent judgement, giving the attacker a gradient to follow rather than a random search.

Added value to my researchDynamic red-teaming is the discipline I want for spatial models too. My explainable-ML work usually reports global SHAP-style attributions on a fixed test set; the analogue here is an adversarial search over geographic covariate space — which neighbourhood profiles make a spatial prediction model most wrong or most inequitable. Porting this paper's audit-as-a-moving-target logic gives me a reusable protocol for stress-testing GeoAI models, and a reproducibility story (versioned, regenerable benchmarks) that fits my FAIR/open-science line.

③ Close the loop: from simulated decisions to real conversations at population scale

Extending this paperAll 61,200 decisions are elicited: the model is prompted by researchers, not by patients. The obvious and highest-value extension is to test whether the dissociation survives contact with reality — take real, de-identified health-query logs, classify which disclose a stigmatized condition (HIV status, HBV, a psychiatric diagnosis), and measure whether downstream model behaviour (hedging, referral to a clinician, refusal, tone, depth of advice) differs from matched queries with a non-stigmatized condition. The paper establishes stigma in the lab; population-scale log analysis would establish its incidence — how often, for how many people, in which query types. It would also test the paper's mitigation claim in the wild, since deployed systems already carry system prompts that may or may not encode the strategies it recommends.

Added value to my researchThis is a direct fit for my multi-source data-fusion and digital-divide work. Health-query logs are a behavioural sensor much like mobile-phone or social-media traces, and they can be geocoded coarsely — so I can ask whether algorithmic quality of health advice varies across the same geography as physical care deserts. If the places with the worst 2SFCA scores also receive the most stigma-congruent AI advice, the digital layer is amplifying rather than substituting for missing care: a testable, policy-relevant hypothesis and a natural next paper on telehealth equity.

④ Cross the condition axis with the demographic axis — and ask about intersectional stacking

Extending this paperThe paper manipulates health condition while holding the person otherwise constant; a parallel literature manipulates race, gender, class and insurance status while holding condition constant (socio-demographic gaps in LLM-guided pain management is the clearest corpus example). Neither crosses the two. The extension is a full condition × demography factorial on the same 51-scenario spine, asking whether the penalties are additive or super-additive — does being described as a low-income rural migrant with hepatitis B cost more than the sum of the migrant penalty and the HBV penalty? Intersectional stacking is where real harm concentrates, and this paper's design is the only one in the corpus with enough decisions per cell to detect an interaction rather than just a main effect.

Added value to my researchInteraction-hunting across nested attributes is what my MGWR work does spatially: main effects with spatially varying coefficients. I would model the stigma-congruent rate as an outcome with condition, demography and setting as covariates and fit geographically-weighted-style interaction terms where the "geography" is the described setting — importing my spatial-non-stationarity machinery into model auditing. It also gives my explainable-ML line a genuinely new dependent variable: not disease prevalence, but algorithmic treatment of the diseased.

⑤ Take the dissociation seriously as a theory of evaluation — and rebuild fairness testing around behaviour

Extending this paperThe deepest claim here is not "LLMs are stigmatizing" but that a model's declared values and its enacted values are separable channels, and that alignment training may have optimized only the first. That deserves a mechanistic follow-up rather than another benchmark: does the gap widen with RLHF intensity? Does it appear because explicit-stigma prompts are recognizably tests while vignettes are not — i.e. is this evaluation-awareness rather than bias? Testing the paper's own explanation would mean probing whether the model represents "this is a fairness question" as a detectable internal state, and whether the mined prompting strategies work by suppressing stigma or merely by re-flagging the scenario as a test. The paper's cross-lingual transfer result is the strongest existing clue that something structural, not surface-lexical, is at play — and its reasoning-mode contrast is the natural experimental handle.

Added value to my researchThe say-do gap is the discipline I most need to import into geospatial foundation models. Fine-tuned GeoFMs are validated on held-out benchmark tiles — the spatial equivalent of an explicit scale — and I have no protocol for asking whether the model behaves equitably when embedded in an allocation decision (siting a clinic, an EV charger, a testing site). This paper hands me the template: pair every benchmark score with a situated-decision audit, and report the gap as a first-class metric. That becomes a methods contribution in its own right — "behavioural validation for GeoAI" — spanning my facility-siting, equity-optimization and open-science threads.
