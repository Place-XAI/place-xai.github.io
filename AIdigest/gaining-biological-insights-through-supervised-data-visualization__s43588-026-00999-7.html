Gaining biological insights through supervised data visualization

Jake S. Rhodes · Adrien Aumon · Sacha Morin · Marc Girard · Catherine Larochelle · Elsa Brunet-Ratnasingham · Amélie Pagliuzza · Lorie Marchitto · Wei Zhang · Adele Cutler · François Grand’Maison · Anhong Zhou · Andrés Finzi · Nicolas Chomont · Daniel E. Kaufmann · Stephanie Zandee · Alexandre Prat · Guy Wolf · Kevin R. Moon Published 2026-06-30 DOI 10.1038/s43588-026-00999-7 Source nature.com ↗
Core Conclusion

Unsupervised embeddings (t-SNE, UMAP, Isomap) show only the dominant structure of a dataset, which frequently is not the structure the analyst cares about. RF-PHATE fixes the mismatch by letting a random forest learn which feature relationships actually predict an expert-provided label, converting those learned relationships into a diffusion geometry and then into a low-dimensional map — so label-relevant structure is amplified and extraneous variation is suppressed. It works for both classification and regression, scales to large datasets, and across four biological case studies (longitudinal multiple sclerosis, Raman spectra of antioxidant effects, COVID-19 patient outcomes, RNA-seq with simulated dropout) it exposes continuous biological gradients that unsupervised maps bury under noise.

Why It Matters

  • Names a failure mode everyone silently tolerates. Nearly every omics, spectroscopy and clinical-cohort paper opens with a t-SNE/UMAP panel. Those maps are optimized for whatever variance dominates — batch, donor, instrument, acquisition day — not for the biological question. The paper turns "my UMAP looks like batch" from folklore into an addressable design problem.
  • Supervision without collapsing the manifold. Prior supervised variants (supervised t-SNE / UMAP / Isomap / LLE) tend to either over-separate classes into artificial islands or destroy continuous structure. RF-PHATE keeps a continuum: in the MS case it reveals gradients of disease trajectory rather than tidy but uninformative label blobs.
  • Regression targets, not just classes. Most supervised embedding work assumes discrete labels. Handling continuous responses matters for dose–response, severity scores, viral load, and any exposure-gradient study.
  • Robustness is demonstrated, not asserted. The RNA-seq case study injects simulated dropout, and the Raman case works on noisy, baseline-drifting spectra — testing the embedding where real biological measurement actually lives.
  • Ships as usable software. A Python implementation is public (jakerhodes/RF-PHATE) with a Zenodo-archived release, so the method is adoptable the day the paper appears.

Core Methods

  • Step 1 — supervised similarity from a random forest. A random forest (Breiman, 2001) is fitted to predict the expert label from the features. The forest's partitioning encodes which feature interactions matter for that label; irrelevant variation is never used to split, so it is implicitly discounted.
  • Step 2 — RF-GAP proximities. Similarity between samples is read out with geometry- and accuracy-preserving random forest proximities (Rhodes, Cutler & Moon, IEEE TPAMI, 2023) rather than naive co-occurrence counts. This is the load-bearing choice: RF-GAP proximities reproduce the forest's own out-of-bag predictions, which keeps the similarity faithful to the fitted model instead of merely to leaf co-membership.
  • Step 3 — diffusion geometry. Proximities become a Markov diffusion operator (Coifman & Lafon, 2006); powering it denoises local noise and reveals multi-scale connectivity — the mechanism that preserves continuous transitions instead of shattering the data into label islands.
  • Step 4 — PHATE potential distances + MDS. Following PHATE (Moon et al., Nat. Biotechnol. 37, 1482–1492, 2019), diffusion probabilities are transformed into an information (potential) distance and embedded by multidimensional scaling into 2–3 dimensions, retaining both local and global structure.
  • Classification and regression supervision. Because the supervision enters only through the forest's objective, continuous targets work with no change of machinery — demonstrated on the hippocampus/regression setting.
  • Scalability. The implementation is designed for large datasets (landmark/approximation strategies in the diffusion + MDS stage), addressing a standard bottleneck of diffusion-based embeddings.
  • Longitudinal handling. The MS analysis compares patient follow-up trajectories with dynamic time warping (Berndt & Clifford; Keogh & Ratanamahatana) so visits sampled at irregular intervals remain comparable in embedding space.
  • Evaluation. Quantitative comparison against t-SNE, UMAP, Isomap, LLE, MDS and their supervised variants, using silhouette scores, distance-weighted k-NN label recovery and embedding-stress diagnostics; group differences tested with the Mann–Whitney U test. Raman spectra are preprocessed by adaptive iteratively reweighted penalized least-squares baseline correction (Zhang et al., 2010).
  • Stress tests. Simulated dropout on RNA-seq and noisy spectral acquisition probe robustness; the synthetic branching tree gives a known-ground-truth check that the method does not manufacture structure.

Expanded Ideas — this paper's extensibility & added value to my research

Each idea stays anchored to this paper: first how RF-PHATE itself could be extended (drawing on our corpus), then the added value for my research lines (migis.net — GeoAI, health-geography accessibility & 2SFCA/R2SFCA, explainable & physics-informed spatial learning, multi-source data fusion).

① Geo-RF-PHATE: make the supervising forest spatially non-stationary

Extending this paperRF-PHATE's supervision is a single global random forest: one feature-importance regime is assumed to hold for every sample. That is exactly the assumption that fails when samples carry coordinates — the features predicting an outcome in a dense urban core differ from those in a rural periphery. A direct extension: fit geographically weighted / locally adaptive forests, derive RF-GAP proximities under local weighting, and let the diffusion operator blend local supervised geometries. The embedding then shows not just "which samples are alike given the label" but "where the label-generating mechanism itself changes" — a spatial-heterogeneity map that no current supervised embedding produces. The paper's artificial-tree control gives a ready-made ground-truth harness: plant two different branching rules in two regions and check that the method recovers the boundary rather than smearing it.

Added value to my researchThis is the missing visual companion to MGWR. My MGWR work returns per-variable bandwidth surfaces that are hard to read jointly; a Geo-RF-PHATE embedding of the local-forest proximity space would cluster neighbourhoods by mechanism regime instead of by outcome level — giving me a defensible way to delineate "spatial process zones" for primary-care and mental-health accessibility studies, and to argue that a single global model is inadequate before I fit one.

② Turn the MS trajectory analysis into a general supervised spatiotemporal trajectory space

Extending this paperFig. 2 is the paper's most consequential result and also its most under-formalized: longitudinal MS visits are embedded and then compared with dynamic time warping, i.e. the temporal logic is bolted on after a static embedding. Extending the paper: make time first-class — supervise the forest on a future outcome (progression at t+Δ), define proximities between trajectory segments rather than visits, and diffuse on a graph whose edges encode both similarity and admissible temporal transitions. That converts the map from a snapshot into a supervised trajectory manifold with directionality, so branch points become candidate prognostic decision moments rather than post-hoc annotations. The COVID-19 case (Fig. 4) would then also gain a course, not just an outcome label.

Added value to my researchDirectly portable to my spatiotemporal work: substitute "patient visit" with "census tract–month" and "EDSS progression" with telehealth-uptake or care-utilization change. A supervised trajectory manifold would let me visualize how neighbourhoods move through digital-divide states over a decade — a far more honest representation than the KDE surfaces and change-detection maps I currently use, and one that naturally exposes tracts sitting at a branch point where policy could still redirect the trajectory.

③ A parametric, out-of-sample RF-PHATE encoder for streaming and city-scale data

Extending this paperRF-PHATE inherits the classic transductive limitation of diffusion + MDS pipelines: the embedding is computed for a fixed sample set, and adding new observations invites recomputation. The paper mitigates cost with scalability engineering, but not with an inductive map. Extension: train a graph neural network on the RF-GAP proximity graph to regress the RF-PHATE coordinates, yielding a parametric encoder that (a) embeds unseen samples in constant time, (b) can be fine-tuned as the forest is retrained, and (c) makes the embedding differentiable so it can be plugged into downstream models. Validation is cheap — hold out entire MSBase sites or entire Raman acquisition batches and test whether the encoder places them where full recomputation would.

Added value to my researchRecomputation cost is precisely why supervised embeddings have not entered my pipelines: mobile-phone, POI and trajectory datasets arrive continuously and are far larger than a clinical cohort. An inductive RF-PHATE encoder makes supervised visualization usable for streaming urban data — new POI batches or new months of mobility land on a stable map, so drift becomes visible as movement rather than as an unrecognizably re-laid-out plot.

Cross-ref (corpus): Accelerating network layouts using graph neural networks — 10.1038/s41467-023-37189-2

④ Multi-label and multi-modal supervision: which label owns which axis?

Extending this paperEvery case study supervises on one label, yet real cohorts carry many simultaneously — MS: disability score, relapse count, treatment line, MRI lesion load; COVID-19: severity, viral load, antibody titre. Extension: fit a multi-output forest (or a set of forests) and either concatenate proximity operators with learned weights or build a partially shared geometry that separates variation common to all labels from variation specific to each. The payoff is diagnostic as much as visual: an explicit decomposition of how much of the embedding a given clinical annotation is responsible for, plus a principled way to fuse modalities (spectra + transcriptome + clinical record) with heterogeneous label availability. This would also answer the sharpest reviewer objection to any supervised embedding — the worry that the map merely re-draws the label you fed it.

Added value to my researchMy data fusion problem is structurally identical: remote sensing, POIs, social media and mobile-phone records with partially overlapping outcome labels (access, utilization, health status). A shared-plus-specific supervised geometry would let me state which fused signal is actually carrying an accessibility outcome and which is redundant — turning "multi-source fusion" from a data-plumbing claim into a measurable contribution.

⑤ Guardrails: make supervised visualization falsifiable before it becomes clinical evidence

Extending this paperA supervised embedding is a persuasive object — separation in the picture looks like discovery, even when it partly reflects the supervision itself. RF-GAP's out-of-bag construction is a real defence, but the paper's own framing ("gaining biological insights") invites a stronger protocol. Extension: ship RF-PHATE with a label-permutation null (embed under shuffled labels and report the separation actually attributable to signal), a held-out embedding convention where the forest never sees the visualized samples, and a stability index across forest seeds and bootstrap resamples. Report these as standard panels beside every map, as effect sizes with confidence intervals are reported beside every group comparison. For the MS case this is not pedantry: a branch point read as a prognostic subgroup will eventually be read as a clinical claim.

Added value to my researchExactly the protocol my explainable-spatial-ML work needs, with a spatial twist: the permutation null must be a spatially constrained one (conditional/torus permutation) or autocorrelation alone will manufacture "significant" structure. Formalizing supervised-embedding guardrails for spatial data is a publishable methods contribution in its own right, and it fits my open-science/FAIR line — a reusable reporting checklist plus reference implementation, not just another map.
